AI & the planet

Every answer is made somewhere. That somewhere is real.

Cloud AI feels weightless — a text box, an instant reply, nothing in your hands. It isn't. It's a building on real land, drinking real water, pulling from a real grid, going up faster than anything else on it. Below is what's actually verified, in the companies' own words. Including the parts that don't flatter us.

The trajectory

Nothing else on the grid is growing like this.

These are the International Energy Agency's numbers — the most sober source there is on this, and the one least likely to be accused of hype.

Growing ~15% a year, more than four times the rate of all other demand combined.

Source: IEA, Energy and AI — “around 415 terawatt hours (TWh), or about 1.5% of global electricity consumption in 2024”, projected to “double to reach around 945 TWh by 2030”.

The part with a postcode
One building drinks
like a small town.

Electricity is abstract. Water isn't — it comes out of a specific reservoir, next to specific people, and evaporative cooling doesn't give it back.

5M gallons

Per day, for one large data centre — about 1.8 billion a year. That's the water use of a town of 10,000 to 50,000 people.

449M gallons

Per day, US data centres combined — 163.7 billion gallons a year, as of 2021, before the AI buildout really began.

519 ml

A single 100-word AI prompt is estimated to use roughly one bottle of water.

700,000 L

Evaporated training GPT-3 in Microsoft's US data centres — about the freshwater used to manufacture 370 BMWs.

Sources: EESI · UC Riverside · “Making AI Less Thirsty”. Disclosure is largely voluntary, so communities often can't find out what's being used next door.
What we're not going to tell you

AI is not why the planet is in trouble.

We sell local AI. It would be convenient for us to end this page with "…and that's why the cloud is killing the Earth." It isn't true, and you'd be right to stop trusting the rest of the page if we said it.

The same IEA report says data centre growth accounts for less than 10% of global electricity demand growth to 2030, and that data centres land at just under 3% of world consumption. Aviation, cement, steel and cattle are not waiting for AI to catch up. Anyone selling you AI as the villain of climate change is selling you something.

And one more, about us. Running a model on your own laptop is not more energy-efficient than running it in a data centre. It's less. Research measuring intelligence per joule found purpose-built cloud chips beat a high-end local machine by roughly 1.6× to 7.4× — and that's at batch size one, so it isn't even the batching advantage. Their hardware is genuinely better at this than yours.

What's actually true is narrower, and we'll only claim this much: no cooling tower evaporates a town's water so you can ask a question, and nothing new gets built because you asked it — your computer already exists, and it's already on.

Sources: IEA · “Intelligence per Watt”, arXiv 2511.07885.
