Best Laptop AI Assistant for Privacy: Local and Offline

By Hector Gras · 2 August 2026

We tested this
  • Can you run AI with 8 GB of RAM? We tried the weakest laptop we have
  • Run AI on a laptop without a graphics card: do Intel graphics help?

The best laptop AI assistant for privacy is one that runs on the laptop itself, so your chats and documents never go to a server. The free options are LM Studio, Jan, Ollama and GPT4All, where you choose and download the model yourself. GreenCube, the one I build, costs €9.99 once and sets itself up for your laptop. Copilot and the ChatGPT app are more capable and already on many laptops, but they answer from the cloud, so they aren't private in the same way.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Here's how the options compare and how to check any of them yourself.

What "local" means for your privacy

A local assistant runs the AI model on your laptop. When you ask about a PDF, the file is read from your disk and the answer is worked out on your processor or graphics card. Nothing about the conversation has to leave the machine.

A cloud assistant, like Copilot in Windows or the ChatGPT desktop app, is a window onto a data centre. Every message and every file you attach goes to the company's servers. That's how they can run much bigger models, and it's also why your chats end up on someone else's computer. Some apps mix the two, so check which mode is active: an app can look local while one step, such as reading a scanned page, quietly uses a cloud service.

The options

GreenCube

A Windows app for people who want a private assistant without setup. You install it, sign in once with Google or Microsoft (only to check you paid), and on first launch it checks your laptop and downloads the model that fits: Cube 2B on smaller laptops, Cube 4B if there's a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma. After that it works offline. It chats, reads PDFs, Word files and photos, and makes slide decks, documents, flashcards, quizzes and images. Chats and files are saved on your laptop. €9.99 once, 15-day refund, no Mac version.

LM Studio and Jan

Free desktop apps where you browse a list of models, download one and chat with it offline. Great if you enjoy choosing models and settings. If terms like "Q4_K_M" mean nothing to you, expect some reading first. Jan is open source; LM Studio isn't.

Ollama and GPT4All

Ollama is a free engine that runs models, mostly from the command line, and other apps plug into it. GPT4All is a free desktop app with document chat built in. Both are good if you're comfortable with a bit of setup. Compared in offline AI apps compared.

Copilot and the ChatGPT app

The most convenient and the most capable, especially on hard questions, and they know today's news. But they need internet and your conversations go to Microsoft or OpenAI. Fine for anything you'd happily post publicly; less so for contracts, health letters or client work. See is ChatGPT private?

What your laptop needs

Memory matters most. About 8 GB of RAM is enough to run a small model, and 16 GB is noticeably better. A graphics card makes a big difference to speed. Here's how fast GreenCube writes on the PCs we tested (reading speed is about 5 tokens a second):

Your laptop or PCWrites
8 GB laptop, no graphics card5–7 tokens/s, about reading speed
16 GB laptop, no graphics card14–18 tokens/s
Gaming PC or laptop with a 6 GB+ card37–76 tokens/s on most cards

Other local apps running similar-sized models land in a similar range. Long PDFs take longer to read on a laptop without a graphics card, and making images needs 12 GB of RAM in GreenCube. Check yours with Can my PC run AI? or read AI on a laptop without a GPU.

How to check an app really is offline

  • After the first model download, turn the Wi-Fi off and use the app normally for a while, including a PDF. If everything works, the AI is running on your laptop.
  • Read what the app says it sends. GreenCube, for example, goes online for the account check and for updates, and for web lookups only if you switch Online on; it never sends a chat, file or image. Other apps send usage data or crash reports, so look for that in the privacy policy.
  • If you want to see it for yourself, watch network activity in Windows' Resource Monitor while the app works on a document.
  • Remember the laptop itself: turn on device encryption or BitLocker and use your own Windows account, so a lost laptop doesn't expose what's on it. More in offline AI security.

What you give up

A model small enough for a laptop is less capable than the ones behind Copilot or ChatGPT. You'll notice it on long chains of reasoning, tricky code and very specific facts. It doesn't know today's news unless you switch on web lookups, and on an older laptop you'll wait for long answers. Plenty of people use both: a cloud assistant for public questions and a local one for documents they'd rather keep to themselves.

Questions

What is the best laptop AI assistant for offline use?

One that runs the model on the laptop: LM Studio, Jan, Ollama or GPT4All if you're happy to choose a model yourself, or GreenCube if you want it set up for you for EUR 9.99 once.

Does a local AI assistant work without internet?

Yes, once the model is downloaded. GreenCube needs internet once to sign in and download the AI; after that the chat, documents and images all work offline.

How much RAM do you need to run a local AI assistant?

About 8 GB to run a small model at reading speed, and 16 GB for two to three times that speed on the laptops we tested. A graphics card helps more than anything else.

How do I verify that an AI app is truly offline?

Turn the Wi-Fi off after the first download and use it, including with a document. If it keeps working, it's local. Then read its privacy page to see what it sends when you're back online.

Is a one-time purchase AI assistant better than a subscription?

It's cheaper over time and you keep it, but a subscription to a cloud AI buys you a much bigger model. Which is better depends on whether you need the strongest AI or a private one you own.
