Best Microsoft Copilot Alternatives for Privacy in 2026

By Hector Gras · 23 July 2026

If you want a Copilot alternative for privacy, the answer is an AI that runs on your own computer instead of in Microsoft's cloud. GreenCube does this without any setup for €9.99 once; LM Studio, Jan, Ollama and GPT4All are free if you're happy to pick and download a model yourself. Once the model is downloaded, all of them work with the Wi-Fi off, and your chats stay on your PC.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Below is why Copilot can't be private in that sense, what the alternatives are, and what you give up.

Why Copilot isn't the private option

Microsoft Copilot is a cloud service. What you type, the files you share with it and the images you upload are sent to Microsoft's servers, and the answer comes back from there. Microsoft publishes policies on how it handles that data, but the data still leaves your computer. For everyday questions that may be fine. For personal finances, medical letters, legal papers or private emails, many people would rather it didn't.

A simple way to tell whether any AI tool is private: switch off your network and try it. If it still answers, the work is happening on your machine. If it can't, your words were going somewhere.

The alternatives that run on your PC

GreenCube

GreenCube is the app I make, for people who would rather not choose models or settings. You install it on Windows 10 or 11 and it checks your PC and picks the AI model that fits: Cube 2B on smaller PCs, Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma. You sign in once with Google or Microsoft, only to check you paid (it never sees your chats or files), and the first launch downloads the model. After that it works offline. It only goes online for updates, or if you switch Online on for web lookups.

It chats, reads PDFs, Word files and photos, and makes slide decks you can save as PowerPoint, documents, flashcards, quizzes, diagrams and images. Your chats and files are saved on your PC. It costs €9.99 once, with no subscription, no message limits and a 15-day refund. There's no Mac version.

LM Studio, Jan, Ollama and GPT4All

These are free. You choose a model from a list, download it and chat with it offline. LM Studio has the most complete desktop window. Jan and GPT4All are open source and simple to start with. Ollama is open source too and built mainly for developers, with a command line and a local API. They're a great fit if you enjoy trying models and settings. If you don't know which model your PC can handle, expect some reading first.

What you give up

Being honest: an AI that fits on a laptop is not as capable as the cloud models behind Copilot. It's good for writing, studying, summarising documents and everyday questions, but weaker on the hardest maths, tricky code and very niche facts. It doesn't know today's news unless you let it look things up online. And it won't sit inside Word, Outlook and Teams the way Copilot does in Microsoft 365.

Questions

What makes an AI tool truly private?

Easy test: switch off your network and try it. If it still answers, the work is happening on your machine, which a privacy policy alone can't tell you.

Is offline AI as capable as Microsoft Copilot?

No. An AI that fits on a laptop is good for writing, studying, summarising documents and everyday questions, but weaker than Copilot's cloud models on the hardest maths, tricky code and niche facts. It also won't sit inside Word, Outlook and Teams.
