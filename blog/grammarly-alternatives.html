The Best Grammarly Alternatives in 2026: Honest Picks

By Hector Gras · 18 July 2026

The best Grammarly alternative depends on what you write. For a free grammar checker, QuillBot. For writing in languages other than English, LanguageTool or DeepL Write. For novels and long reports, ProWritingAid. For plainer, clearer sentences, Hemingway Editor. And if your drafts are private, an AI that runs on your own computer, like GreenCube, keeps the text off anyone's server.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. It isn't a grammar checker in the usual sense, so it gets one short section near the end. Everything else here is a real writing tool, grouped by the job it does best. Free plans change often, so check each tool's current limits before you rely on them.

Grammar checkers like Grammarly

QuillBot has a free grammar checker that PCMag recommends as a free swap for Grammarly, plus a paraphraser and translation. It's a good first stop for students and English learners. Its grammar feedback is lighter than the paid checkers.

LanguageTool checks more than 30 languages and dialects. It has add-ons for the main browsers and works in Microsoft Word. The free version covers spelling, basic punctuation and some style; Premium adds the full rule set.

ProWritingAid goes deeper on craft than any of the others: its reports look at pacing, sentence variety, overused words and dialogue, not only commas. That's useful for novelists and long-form writers. The number of reports takes time to learn, and it's more than you need for emails.

Ginger checks grammar and can read your text back aloud, which helps if you catch mistakes better by ear. WhiteSmoke combines grammar, translation and plagiarism checks, and is paid only. Slick Write is free and needs no sign-up. SentenceCheckup is a quick free checker for single sentences, and Virtual Writing Tutor is a free checker aimed at people learning English.

Clarity and readability

Hemingway Editor doesn't correct you so much as point at problems: long, hard sentences, passive voice and adverbs are highlighted, and you fix them yourself. Writers who want to sharpen their own editing like that. It doesn't do much grammar correction, and it's English only. The web version is free.

Readable gives readability scores for your text, useful if you write for a broad audience and want a number to aim at.

Rewriting and tone

Wordtune offers several rewrites of each sentence, which helps when something reads awkwardly but you can't say why. Non-native writers often find it useful. The free plan is limited.

Rewriters change phrasing and tone but can miss real grammar errors, and grammar checkers catch errors but won't reword your prose. Many people end up using one of each.

Writing in other languages

DeepL Write pairs grammar and style correction with DeepL's translation, a strong choice for business writers who draft in more than one language. Reverso adds a dictionary with examples in context and verb conjugation, which suits language learners and translators. Along with LanguageTool, these are the ones to test first if you don't write mainly in English; most other tools treat other languages as an afterthought.

For editors and teams

PerfectIt is a Word add-in that checks a whole document for consistent style. It's built for professional editors and is overkill for casual writing. Writer is for companies that want everyone to follow one brand voice, and it's priced for organisations, not individuals. Sapling is built for customer support teams, suggesting replies and fixes as agents type.

Plagiarism checkers

Copyleaks uses AI to detect copied text across many file types, which suits publishers and content managers. Quetext shows matches in colour-coded results that are easy to scan, but its grammar help is limited. PaperRater combines grammar feedback and a plagiarism check for free, aimed at students.

AI writers and chatbots

ChatGPT can fix grammar, rewrite paragraphs and suggest structure in one place. It can also introduce errors or change your meaning, especially in long documents, so read its edits rather than accepting them blind. EditGPT uses the same kind of AI but shows the changes as edits you can review. Jasper and Writesonic are mainly for producing marketing copy from templates; they're not the right tool if you mostly edit your own writing.

Will a grammar checker get you flagged for AI?

Fixing spelling and commas leaves your writing yours, so it's unlikely to look machine-written. Rewriting whole paragraphs with a paraphraser or a chatbot is different, because then many of the words are the tool's. If your school or job has rules about AI, use checkers for corrections and do the rewriting yourself.

How to choose

  • Does it work in the apps you already use, like Word, Google Docs or your browser?
  • Does it support the language, or the variety of English, you write in?
  • Does the free plan cover the length of what you usually write?
  • Does it send your text to a server, and does that matter for what you write?
  • Do you mainly need corrections, rewrites, or both?

A slightly weaker checker that lives inside your usual editor will get used. A stronger one that makes you copy text into a separate window often won't.

Questions

Is there a better option than Grammarly?

It depends on what you write. QuillBot has a good free checker, LanguageTool and DeepL Write are better if you write in other languages, ProWritingAid goes deeper on long-form craft, and Hemingway Editor helps with clarity.

Is ChatGPT or Grammarly better for grammar checking?

Grammarly is the more dependable choice for catching mechanical errors as you type. ChatGPT is more flexible for rewriting, tone and structure, but it can introduce errors or change your meaning, so read its edits.

Will I get flagged for AI if I use a grammar checker?

Fixing spelling and commas leaves your writing yours, so it's unlikely to look machine-written. Rewriting whole paragraphs with a paraphraser or a chatbot is different, because many of the words are then the tool's; if your school or job has rules about AI, do the rewriting yourself.

What is the most private grammar tool available?

A tool that runs on your own computer, because your text never goes to a server. GreenCube is one: a Windows chat app that runs the AI on your PC and works offline after setup, though it isn't a checker that underlines words as you type.

What's the best free Grammarly substitute for students?

QuillBot's free grammar checker is a good first stop for students. PaperRater adds a free plagiarism check alongside its grammar feedback.
