Offline AI: Why It Matters and How to Get Started

By Hector Gras · 25 August 2026

Offline AI is worth having when you want your chats and files to stay on your own computer, when you need it to work without Wi-Fi, or when you're tired of paying every month. The catch is that a model small enough to run on a laptop is less capable than the big cloud AIs on the hardest problems. For everyday writing, studying and questions about your own documents, most people find that trade is fine.

I build GreenCube, an offline AI app, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. This post covers what "offline" really means, where it helps, where the cloud is still better, and how to try it.

What "offline AI" actually means

An offline AI does its thinking on your device. The model is a file on your drive, and a program on your computer runs it on your processor or graphics card (the open-source llama.cpp is behind many of these apps). Nothing has to go out for an answer to come back.

That's different from an app that merely copes with a bad connection. Plenty of AI apps show your old chats offline but can't write a new word until they reach a server. The easy test is airplane mode: switch the Wi-Fi off and ask something new.

What you gain

Privacy you don't have to take on trust

When the model runs on your PC, your prompts aren't stored on someone else's server, so they can't leak from it, be read by staff or be used to train the next model. You aren't relying on a privacy policy, because there's no company in the loop. That matters most for contracts, health notes, client files or a diary.

It works anywhere

On a plane, on a train through tunnels, in a library with weak Wi-Fi, or anywhere the internet is slow or expensive. An offline AI behaves the same with or without a connection.

No meter running

Cloud AI is usually free with limits, or paid monthly. A local model has no per-message cost and no daily cap: once it's on your PC you can use it as much as you like. You pay in electricity and in hardware you already own.

Speed depends on your PC

There's no network round trip, so answers start straight away. How fast the words come after that depends on your hardware. On the PCs we tested, GreenCube wrote 5–7 tokens a second on an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card (about reading speed), 14–18 on a 16 GB laptop, and 37–76 on most gaming PCs with a 6 GB or bigger card. Cloud services run on data-centre hardware, so on a modest laptop they will often feel faster.

Where offline AI makes the most sense

  • Sensitive documents: contracts, patient notes, tax files, anything you'd rather not upload.
  • Studying: summarising notes or making flashcards and quizzes from a PDF, without depending on campus Wi-Fi.
  • Travel and fieldwork, where the signal comes and goes.
  • Places where the internet is slow or costly, including schools that can't count on a good connection.
  • Everyday drafting and questions, if you'd simply rather not keep a record of your chats on someone else's server.

In each case the connection itself is the problem, whether that's a dead zone, a rule at work, or a client who wants their files to stay put.

How a model fits on a laptop

The models behind ChatGPT and Gemini are far too big for a home computer. Offline apps use smaller models, usually a few billion parameters, and store them compressed. That compression, called quantization, keeps each of the model's numbers in fewer bits (often about 4 instead of 16), which makes the file several times smaller for a modest loss in quality. The usual file format for this is GGUF.

Memory is the main limit. As a rough guide, a small model runs on 8 GB of RAM, a mid-size one wants 16 GB, and a graphics card with its own memory makes everything much faster. If you're not sure about your machine, Can my PC run AI? checks it for you.

When the cloud is still the better choice

  • The hardest problems: long chains of maths, tricky code, very niche facts. The big cloud models are clearly stronger here.
  • Today's news. A local model only knows what it learned before it was released, unless the app can search the web.
  • Very long documents or huge batches of work, where data-centre hardware simply has more room.

There are smaller costs too. The first download is a few gigabytes, an old laptop will be slow and warm, and updates arrive when you install them rather than silently overnight. Plenty of people use both: the cloud for the hard questions, an offline AI for private work and for when the Wi-Fi is gone. There's a fuller comparison in offline AI vs cloud AI.

How to get started

Questions

What is the 30% rule in AI?

There isn't one agreed rule. People use "30%" loosely, for example for how much of a job AI might take over, but it's not a technical standard, so be careful with any specific claim about it.

Would AI work without internet?

Yes, if the model is on your device. Cloud apps like ChatGPT stop working offline, but a local AI app keeps answering once its model is downloaded.

How do you make AI work offline?

Install an app that runs models on your computer and let it download one that fits your memory, usually a few gigabytes. After that first download it needs no connection.
