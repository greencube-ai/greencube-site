Top 3 Offline AI Solutions: Ollama Alternatives 2026

By Hector Gras · 16 July 2026

If Ollama feels too technical, three offline alternatives are worth a look. GreenCube is for people who want it to work without any setup: it picks the AI model for your PC and costs €9.99 once. Atomic Chat is for tinkerers who want lots of models and a local API. Sanctum is aimed at private chat with your documents. All three run the AI on your own computer, so your files stay there.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Below is what each one is good at, where it falls short, and the free apps people usually compare with Ollama too.

Why people look past Ollama

Ollama is free and open source (MIT licence). It started as a tool for developers: you run models from a command line and talk to them through a local API, and since July 2025 it also has a simple chat window. It's excellent if you're comfortable in a terminal or want to plug local models into your own code. If you just want to open an app, drop in a PDF and ask questions, it can feel like the wrong tool. There's a direct comparison in Ollama vs GreenCube and Ollama vs LM Studio.

1. GreenCube: no setup, pay once

GreenCube is the app I build, for people who want the result without learning about models. You install it on Windows 10 or 11, and it checks your PC and picks the model that fits: Cube 2B on smaller PCs, Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma. You sign in once (only to check you paid) and the first launch downloads the model. After that it works offline.

Besides chatting, it reads PDFs, Word files and photos, and makes slide decks, documents, flashcards, quizzes, diagrams and images, all on your computer. Your chats and files are saved on your PC. It costs €9.99 once, with no subscription and a 15-day refund.

The catch: you don't choose the model. If you want to try dozens of models and tune settings, Ollama or Atomic Chat give you that and GreenCube doesn't. It's also Windows only, and speed depends on your hardware. On the PCs we tested it wrote 5 to 7 tokens a second on an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card, 14 to 18 on a 16 GB laptop, and 37 to 76 on most gaming PCs with a 6 GB or bigger card.

2. Atomic Chat: many models, made for tinkerers

Atomic Chat runs open models such as Llama, Qwen, Gemma and DeepSeek on your device, and says it supports over a thousand of them. It has one-click model setup, easy switching between models, and a local API that works with OpenAI-style tools, which is handy if you script things. It's open source, and it uses a compression method it calls TurboQuant to fit bigger models in less memory.

It suits people who enjoy comparing models and building small automations. If you don't want to think about which model to run, it asks more of you than GreenCube does.

3. Sanctum: private chat with your documents

Sanctum runs open-source models locally on macOS and Windows and focuses on chatting with your PDFs and other files. It encrypts what it stores on your device and can pull models from Hugging Face, so you have some choice. It aims to be easy to install, though swapping models or changing advanced settings takes some know-how. There's no Linux version.

It suits people who handle legal, financial or research files and want them to stay on their laptop, and who don't mind a bit of setup.

The free apps people also compare

LM Studio, Jan and GPT4All are the other names that come up. All are free: you pick a model from a list, download it and chat with it offline. LM Studio has the most complete desktop window and is free for work too; Jan and GPT4All are open source. They're great if you like choosing models; if terms like quantisation mean nothing to you, expect some reading first. See LM Studio alternatives and offline AI chatbots compared for the longer list.

What any offline AI gives up

A model that fits on a laptop is smaller than the ones behind ChatGPT or Claude, so it's weaker on the hardest maths, tricky code and very niche facts. It also doesn't know recent news. In return it works on a plane, has no message caps, and keeps your files on your own computer. Before you pick, check what your PC can run with Can my PC run AI?
