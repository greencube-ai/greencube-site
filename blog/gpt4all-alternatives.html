What's going on with GPT4All

GPT4All, by Nomic, was one of the first easy desktop apps for running AI on your own computer: free, MIT-licensed, with LocalDocs for chatting with your files (GitHub). Its releases page lists v3.10.0 from 25 February 2025 as the latest desktop release (releases). Nomic hasn't announced an end to the project on the pages we read, so we won't call it dead, only unupdated.

Why that matters: new AI model families usually need a newer engine to load, and every month of updates brings speed and bug fixes. An app that stops updating keeps working, but it falls further behind with each new model.

Pick by what you used GPT4All for

A free, open-source desktop chat

Jan is the closest swap: a desktop chat app that runs models itself, with a local OpenAI-compatible server, under Apache 2.0, for Windows, Mac with Apple chip and Linux (GitHub). Ollama (MIT) is a command-line engine with a desktop chat window on Windows and Mac. LM Studio is free too, but closed source.

LocalDocs: chatting with your own files

AnythingLLM (MIT) is built around document chat: PDF, TXT and DOCX, on Windows, Mac and Linux, with a Docker version for teams (GitHub). Jan, LM Studio and Msty also read your files.

Windows, without choosing models

GreenCube reads your graphics card and RAM and downloads the AI that fits, then answers questions about your PDFs, makes study guides and flashcards, reads photos and makes pictures, offline. €9.99 once, Windows 10 and 11. In our PDF test it answered 11 of 12 questions about long documents right, in about 15 seconds each, on a 16 GB laptop.

A Mac with an Intel chip

GPT4All supported macOS 12.6+. Jan and LM Studio need an Apple chip; Ollama's docs list Intel Macs as supported, CPU only, on macOS 14 or newer (Ollama docs).

How fast will it run?

Speed comes from your PC, not the app: the model runs on your graphics card or processor. We measured GreenCube on 10 real PCs: 5–7 tokens a second on an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card (about reading speed), 14–18 on a 16 GB laptop, and 37–76 on most gaming cards with 6 GB or more. How we checked

On 21 October 2026 we read each app's own website, docs, GitHub page and privacy page, and on 23 October we re-checked GPT4All's releases and Mac support for Jan and Ollama. Third-party sites disagree on the year of GPT4All's last release; we go by its own releases page. We make GreenCube, so we've stuck to facts anyone can check and haven't ranked the apps. We'll check again every month, and if GPT4All ships a new release we'll say so at the top.

Questions

Is GPT4All still maintained?

Its GitHub releases page shows v3.10.0, published 25 February 2025, as the latest desktop release when we checked in October 2026. The app still installs and works offline, but it hasn't had a new release since then.

What is the best GPT4All alternative?

For a free, open-source desktop app like GPT4All, Jan (Apache 2.0) is the closest. For chatting with your own documents like GPT4All's LocalDocs, AnythingLLM (MIT) is built around exactly that. On Windows, GreenCube (€9.99 once) picks the model for you and reads PDFs and photos.

Is there a free alternative to GPT4All?

Yes: Jan, LM Studio, Ollama, AnythingLLM, KoboldCpp and Atomic Chat are free, and Msty has a free plan.

What replaces GPT4All LocalDocs?

Most alternatives can read your files: AnythingLLM (PDF, TXT, DOCX), Jan (PDF and Office files), LM Studio (PDF, DOCX, TXT), Msty (Knowledge Stacks) and GreenCube (PDFs, notes, photos). The reading happens on your PC.

Is GPT4All safe to keep using?

It still runs offline and its docs say no data leaves your machine, with chat sharing off by default. The risk of an app without updates is that newer AI models and fixes don't arrive, so over time it falls behind the apps that are still updated.
