Not Private by Default: ChatGPT Privacy in 2026 and How to Protect Your Data

By Hector Gras · 1 September 2026

We checked this
  • Which AI doesn't train on your chats? We checked 9 (October 2026)

Not by default. Everything you type into ChatGPT goes to OpenAI's servers and is saved in your history, and on personal accounts your chats can be used to train future models unless you switch that off. Deleting a chat isn't instant either: OpenAI keeps deleted chats for up to 30 days, longer if the law requires it. That doesn't make ChatGPT dangerous, but it does mean protecting your information is up to you.

I build GreenCube, an AI that runs offline on your PC, so I have an obvious interest here. Everything below about ChatGPT comes from how OpenAI describes its own settings; check its privacy page too, because the details change.

What ChatGPT keeps

Whatever you type or upload is stored: the chats themselves, and any files, images or documents you add. OpenAI also keeps account details (your email, and billing if you pay) and the usual technical data, such as your IP address, device and browser.

So don't paste in ID numbers, medical records, bank details or a client's contract unless you're comfortable with that being stored on someone else's server. If you want to see what's been kept, you can request an export of your data from the settings.

How long it keeps it, and whether it trains on it

On personal accounts, free or paid, the setting that lets OpenAI use your chats to improve its models is on until you turn it off. It's opt-out, not opt-in. Turning it off covers chats from then on; it doesn't undo what earlier chats already contributed. Stanford researchers looked at this across the big chatbot companies and found training on users' chats is a common default.

Your history stays until you delete it. Deleted chats are removed from OpenAI's systems within 30 days, unless they have to be kept for security or legal reasons. That last part is real: a court order in a lawsuit can require a company to keep conversations it would otherwise delete.

What the privacy controls actually do

The controls are worth using, as long as you know where they stop.

  • Temporary Chat keeps a conversation out of your history and out of training. OpenAI may still keep a copy for up to 30 days to check for abuse.
  • "Improve the model for everyone", under Data Controls, is the training switch. Off means future chats aren't used for training.
  • Memory can be cleared or switched off, separately from the training setting.
  • Two-step login stops someone with your password from reading your history.

None of them change the basic fact: your message still travels to OpenAI's servers to be answered. The settings decide what happens to it afterwards, not whether it makes the trip.

Can someone see my ChatGPT chats?

In some situations, yes. Authorised OpenAI staff and contractors can review conversations, for example ones flagged for abuse or tied to a support request. On a work account, your organisation's admins may have access to the workspace's content, depending on how it's set up. Anyone who uses your logged-in device can open your history, and anyone you send a shared link to can read that chat. Courts and law enforcement can require OpenAI to keep or hand over data through legal process.

Secure is not the same as private

ChatGPT traffic is encrypted on the way, and stored data is encrypted on OpenAI's side. That protects you from someone snooping on café Wi-Fi or stealing a hard drive. It doesn't stop the company that holds the keys from reading, reviewing or training on what you sent, within its own rules. Encryption describes how data travels and sits, not who is allowed to look at it.

Does it change by plan?

Yes. Personal accounts (Free, Plus, Pro) work as described above, with training on until you switch it off. OpenAI's business and enterprise plans don't train on your chats by default, but your employer's admins control the workspace, so the privacy question shifts from OpenAI to your company. The API, which developers use, isn't used for training by default either, and eligible business customers can ask for zero data retention.

How to cut your exposure

  1. Redact names, numbers and addresses before you paste, or describe the sensitive part in your own words.
  2. Use Temporary Chat for one-off questions about something personal, like a health worry or a legal draft.
  3. Switch off training in Data Controls, knowing it only covers future chats.
  4. Turn on two-step login.
  5. Export what you want to keep, then delete old chats and accounts you no longer use.

For confidential work, the stronger options are a business plan with a no-training contract, or an AI that runs on your own computer.

Questions

Can Someone See My ChatGPT Chats?

In some cases. Authorised OpenAI staff can review flagged conversations, admins can see content on some work accounts, and anyone with your logged-in device or a shared link can read your chats.

Is ChatGPT Confidential?

Not by default. Chats are stored, and on personal accounts they can be used for training unless you turn that off. Treat anything you type as something another person could one day see.

Can Your ChatGPT Chats Be Leaked?

Data is encrypted in transit and at rest, which protects against outsiders listening in. The bigger risks are someone getting into your account, a shared link going further than you meant, and legal disclosure.

Can I Trust ChatGPT With My Data?

For everyday, non-sensitive tasks most people do, especially with training switched off. For personal identifiers, financial records or client work, use a business plan with a no-training contract or an AI that runs on your own computer.

Is ChatGPT FERPA Compliant?

FERPA is about how US schools handle education records, so the question is really whether your school has approved a tool and signed an agreement for it. Don't put student records into a personal ChatGPT account; ask your school first.
