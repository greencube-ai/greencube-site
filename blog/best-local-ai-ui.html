Pay €9.99 Once for GreenCube, a Private Local AI UI for Windows

By Hector Gras · 11 September 2026

The best local AI UI depends on how much setup you want. If you enjoy choosing models and changing settings, the free apps (LM Studio, Jan, GPT4All, or Ollama with Open WebUI on top) are very good. If you'd rather install one thing and start working without picking a model at all, that's the job GreenCube was built for: a Windows app that chooses the AI for your PC, runs it offline and costs €9.99 once.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Below is what a local AI UI actually does, how the main options differ, and what your PC needs to run any of them.

What a local AI UI does

A local AI model is a file on your disk, usually a few gigabytes. On its own it does nothing. You need a program that loads it into memory, passes it your messages and shows you the answers, and that program is the UI. Many of them use the same engine underneath (llama.cpp or something built on it), so the real differences are in how much they ask of you and what they can do besides chat.

Once the model is on your disk, any of these apps keeps working with the Wi-Fi off, and your chats are saved on your computer instead of on a company's server.

The main options

LM Studio

A free desktop app for Windows, Mac and Linux. You search for models inside the app, download the ones you like and chat with them. It can also run a local server so other programs can use the model. It suits people who like to compare models. The catch is that you choose the model, its size and its quantization yourself, so expect some reading first. More in LM Studio alternatives.

Ollama with Open WebUI

Ollama runs models from the command line and in the background; Open WebUI is a chat page in your browser that sits on top of it and looks a lot like ChatGPT. Developers like this pair, and so do people who host one AI for a whole household or team. It's two separate pieces to install and keep updated. See Ollama vs LM Studio and Open WebUI alternatives.

Jan and GPT4All

Both are free desktop apps with a clean chat window. GPT4All can also chat with a folder of your own documents. They're a gentler start than Ollama, but you still pick and download the model yourself. There's a longer list in offline AI apps compared.

GreenCube

GreenCube is for people who want the result without the setup. When you install it, it checks your PC and picks the model that fits: Cube 2B on smaller PCs, Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma. Besides chat, it makes slide decks (saved as PowerPoint), documents, quizzes, flashcards, labelled diagrams and images, and it reads PDFs, Word files and photos. It runs on Windows 10 and 11 and costs €9.99 (US$9.99), paid once, with a 15-day refund if it isn't for you.

What your PC needs

Any modern Windows PC with about 8 GB of RAM can run a small model. A graphics card makes a big difference. Here's how fast GreenCube wrote on the PCs we tested; reading speed is about 5 tokens a second.

What stays on your PC, and what doesn't

With a local UI, the chatting itself happens on your computer. In GreenCube's case, chats, files and images are saved on your PC. It goes online to sign you in once (with Google or Microsoft, only to check you paid; it never sees your chats or files), to download the AI on first launch, and for updates. It also goes online if you switch on "Online" for web lookups, which is optional. The free apps work the same way: they need the internet to download models, and chatting works offline.

A local app removes the server from the picture, but not the laptop. If your PC could be lost or stolen, turn on Windows device encryption (BitLocker), and keep your chat folders out of OneDrive or Dropbox sync if the content is sensitive.

When a cloud AI is the better choice

The big cloud AIs like ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are smarter than anything that fits on a laptop, and the gap shows on the hardest problems: long maths, tricky code, very niche facts. They also know today's news, which a local model doesn't unless it can search. And a slow PC is a slow PC; no app changes that.

A local UI wins when the material is private, the Wi-Fi is bad or missing, or you're tired of monthly bills and message limits. Plenty of people use both: a cloud AI for the hardest questions, and a local one for drafts, notes and files they'd rather keep to themselves.

Yes. You pay EUR 9.99 (US$9.99) once, with no subscription, credits or message limit after that.

Yes, after the first launch. It needs internet once to sign in (just to check you paid) and to download the AI, then only for updates or if you switch on Online for web lookups.

Which GreenCube model should I choose?

You don't have to: GreenCube checks your PC and picks Cube 2B on smaller PCs or Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma.

Can GreenCube read images and scanned documents?
