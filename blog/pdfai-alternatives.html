PDF.ai alternatives: 12 tools to chat with your PDFs (some fully offline)

By Hector Gras · 9 October 2026

We checked this
  • AI to summarize your notes: which to pick for limits and privacy (2026)

The best PDF.ai alternative depends on whether your PDFs can go to someone else's server. If they can, ChatGPT and Claude handle broad questions well, NotebookLM is best for juggling several sources, and ChatPDF or AskYourPDF are quick for one file. If they can't, you want a tool that reads the file on your own computer: PDFgear for offline OCR and editing, or a local AI app such as GreenCube for asking questions about the document with the Wi-Fi off.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Below are twelve tools, what each is good for, and the catch with each.

Decide this first: cloud or local

PDF.ai, like most "chat with your PDF" sites, uploads your file to a server, runs the AI there and sends back the answer. That's fast and often capable, but your document now exists somewhere outside your control, at least for a while. For a public research paper that's fine. For a signed contract, a medical letter or a client's records, it's worth thinking twice.

A local tool reads the file from your disk and never uploads it. The trade-off is that a model small enough to run on a laptop is weaker than the big cloud models, especially across very long documents or many files at once.

Before trusting a cloud tool with anything sensitive, check three things on its privacy page: whether it keeps your files after the session, whether it uses them to train models, and what the account is for. These policies change, so check the current version rather than an old review.

Twelve PDF.ai alternatives

GreenCube

The app I make. It runs an AI on your Windows PC, so your PDFs, Word files and photos are read from your disk and the chats are saved there too. Besides answering questions about a document, it can turn it into a summary, flashcards, a quiz or slides. It costs €9.99 once. You sign in once so we can check you paid and the first launch downloads the AI; after that it works offline. The catch: it's slower and less sharp than ChatGPT or Claude on long, complicated documents, and it's Windows only.

ChatGPT

Accepts file uploads and handles questions across several documents well, backed by a large general model. It's a cloud tool, so your files are processed on OpenAI's servers, and the free plan has limits on uploads (ChatGPT's free limits).

Claude

Good at reading long documents carefully and sticking to what they say. Also cloud, and the free plan runs out quickly with big files (Claude's usage limits). Anthropic publishes how much text paid plans can take in at once.

Google NotebookLM

Built around notebooks that hold many sources at once, so it fits a project where you're pulling threads across ten papers rather than reading one. It runs on Google's servers. More options in NotebookLM alternatives.

ChatPDF

Drop in a file and start asking questions, no account needed for a quick go. Good for a one-off task, less so for an ongoing project. Cloud.

AskYourPDF

Similar idea: upload one file, ask questions, get answers. Simple, without the research features of a notebook tool. Cloud.

Denser

Focuses on answers tied to the exact passage they came from, which matters for research or reviewing contracts. Whatever tool you use for this, click through and check that the citation points at real text, not a paraphrase.

PDFgear

A desktop PDF app with OCR and editing that work without uploading the file. A good fit if what you mostly need is to fix, convert or read scanned PDFs. If you use its AI chat, check whether that part goes online.

Adobe Acrobat AI Assistant

AI summaries on top of the most complete PDF editor. A natural choice for teams that already mark up, redact and sign in Acrobat. The AI part runs in Adobe's cloud.

Foxit AI Assistant

The same editing-plus-AI idea as Acrobat from a cheaper PDF editor. Worth a look if Acrobat's price puts you off.

Smallpdf

Less a chat tool than a toolkit: convert, compress, merge and edit, with some AI features added. Cloud based.

SciSpace

Made for academic reading, with search and citation features for working through published papers. See also AI tools for researchers.

There are many more in the same three groups (chat with a PDF, edit and convert, research and cite), such as Humata, ChatDOC, iLovePDF and Scholarcy. If you want local document chat for free and don't mind some setup, apps like AnythingLLM do it too (AnythingLLM alternatives).

How to choose in five minutes

  1. Ask whether the file would cause a problem if it leaked. If yes, stay local.
  2. If you need to quote the exact paragraph, pick a tool that shows passage-level citations.
  3. Check the free plan's page and upload limits against your longest document.
  4. Run one scanned page through it. Tools that read typed PDFs well can still stumble on scans.
  5. Check that you can get your notes out in the format you need.

Questions

Which AI works best with PDFs?

ChatGPT, Claude and NotebookLM are the strongest for broad questions across several public documents. When the file can't leave your computer, use a local tool, knowing it's weaker on very long documents.

Is UPDF free to use?

We haven't tried UPDF, so check its pricing page for what the free version includes today.

What are some alternatives to PDF.ai?

ChatPDF and AskYourPDF for quick questions on one file, NotebookLM for many sources, ChatGPT and Claude for general reasoning, Acrobat or Foxit if you also edit PDFs, and PDFgear or GreenCube if the file has to stay on your PC.

What is the best software for creating PDFs?

Editors like Acrobat, Foxit and PDFgear, or online converters like Smallpdf; that's a different job from chatting with PDFs. Wikipedia keeps a long list of PDF software.

Does GreenCube keep my documents private?

Yes. It reads your PDFs and saves your chats on your own computer, and the sign-in only checks that you paid, never sending a chat or file. To check, turn the Wi-Fi off after the first download: it keeps working.
