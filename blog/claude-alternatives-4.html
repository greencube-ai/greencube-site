Top 4 Claude Alternatives for 2026

By Hector Gras · 17 July 2026

If you like Claude for working with documents but want your files kept on your own computer, four alternatives are worth a look: GreenCube (a Windows app, €9.99 once), Kind by Synsira (searches your own files, with a fully on-device version), Elephas (a Mac assistant with an on-device mode) and PeekabooLabs (on-device AI for Korean businesses). None of them is as smart as Claude; what they offer is privacy and offline use.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Claude is excellent at reading long documents and writing about them, and nothing that runs on a laptop matches it on hard problems. People usually look for an alternative because they can't or won't upload certain files, or because they're tired of usage limits (how Claude's limits work). That's what this list is for.

GreenCube

GreenCube is the app I build. It runs an AI on your own Windows PC: it checks your machine, downloads the model that fits (Cube 2B on smaller PCs, Cube 4B with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma) and then works offline. It reads PDFs, Word files and photos, and besides answering questions it makes slide decks that save as PowerPoint, documents, quizzes, flashcards, labelled diagrams and pictures. Your chats and files are saved on your PC.

It costs €9.99 (US$9.99) once, with no subscription and no message limits. The catches: it's Windows only, you sign in once so it can check you paid, and on a slow PC it's slow. On the PCs we tested, an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card wrote at about reading speed, and a gaming PC many times faster.

Kind by Synsira

Kind is a desktop app that answers questions using only the files you give it, and points you to where in those files the answer came from. That makes it handy for a personal archive of notes, scans and documents. There's a free version with limits. According to BetaKit's report on the launch, the original paid plan sends parts of your data to the cloud, and in July 2026 Synsira launched Kind Local Pro, which does all the processing on your computer.

It suits people who mainly want to search and question their own collection rather than chat about anything. The catch is that it's new, most of what's written about it comes from the company, and you need to pick the local version if keeping everything on your machine is the point.

Elephas

Elephas is an AI assistant for Mac, with iPhone and iPad apps. It builds a searchable knowledge base from your documents and works inside other Mac apps, so you can summarise or rewrite where you're typing. It can run AI on your Mac, or connect to cloud providers when you want a bigger model, and it can strip sensitive details before anything goes to the cloud.

It suits lawyers, consultants and researchers on a Mac. The catches are that it's Mac only, it's a subscription, and it only stays fully on your machine in its on-device mode.

PeekabooLabs

PeekabooLabs is a Korean company that builds AI which runs on personal computers without external servers, aimed at businesses with sensitive documents. Its products are tuned for Korean, and the company says they handle Korean business language better than general models.

It suits Korean companies, legal teams and public bodies that need document search to stay on their own machines. For individuals, or anyone working mostly in other languages, it isn't the right fit, and there's little independent review of it.

What you give up compared with Claude

All four run smaller models than Claude, or keep the biggest models behind a cloud option. Expect weaker answers on long, demanding reasoning, and no web search unless the tool offers it. In return, your files stay on your computer and the AI keeps working without a connection. If you're comfortable choosing your own models, free apps like Jan and LM Studio are also worth knowing about; there's a longer list in offline AI chatbots compared.
