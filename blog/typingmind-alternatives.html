Local $9.99 Option: TypingMind Alternatives, Hidden BYOK Costs

By Hector Gras · 9 September 2026

TypingMind alternatives fall into three groups. Managed platforms like Poe give you many models for one subscription, with no API keys to handle. Self-hosted, open-source tools like LibreChat and Open WebUI give you full control if you can run a server. And local apps like GreenCube or Jan run the AI on your own computer, so nothing goes to an AI company at all. Which is best depends on whether you care most about the newest models, control, or privacy.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Here's each real option, who it suits, and the catch.

Why people leave TypingMind

TypingMind sells you the chat interface, and you bring your own API key from OpenAI, Anthropic or another provider, paying them per token. That's cheap if you use it lightly. With long conversations or heavy daily use the per-token bills add up, and nothing caps them. Your messages also still go to whichever provider you connected.

Before switching, look at your last month of API charges on your provider's billing page and compare that with a flat subscription. If your key costs less, the switch may not be worth it for money alone.

Managed platforms (no keys)

Poe gives you a long list of models and ready-made bots under one subscription, with nothing to set up. Good if you want to try many models without accounts everywhere (Poe alternatives).

Aymo AI bundles several models into one subscription with shared team workspaces, for groups who don't want to hand an API key to every member.

Krater is a workspace for text, images and more, with the models built in, aimed at teams that don't want to assemble their own set-up.

T3Chat keeps the interface fast and minimal, with a free tier and a subscription. It suits students and casual users who just want quick answers.

The catch for all four: a monthly bill, and your conversations are processed by the provider.

Self-hosted, open source

LibreChat is the open-source option most people land on. You run it on your own server, so you own the data and can customise everything, and the software costs nothing. Someone has to install it, keep it patched and back it up.

Open WebUI is similar in spirit, popular with developers who want one interface for both local models and API models. Same trade: full control, your own upkeep.

AnythingLLM is built around chatting with your documents. It's free and open source to run yourself, with a paid hosted version if you'd rather not.

If you connect these to a cloud API, your messages still reach that provider. They're only fully private when paired with a model running on your own hardware.

Local apps

Jan is a free, open-source chat app that runs models on your computer. You pick and download the model yourself. It's light and simple, without much in the way of document tools.

Cherry Studio is a desktop client that mixes local and API models and has a built-in knowledge base for your reference files. Good if you work from a lot of documents and like to configure things.

GreenCube is the one I make. It's a Windows app that picks the model for your PC (Cube 2B on smaller PCs, Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma), so there are no keys and no model choices. It reads PDFs, Word files and photos, and makes slide decks, documents, flashcards, quizzes and images. Your chats are saved on your PC. You sign in once to check you paid, the first launch downloads the model, and then it works offline. It costs €9.99 once, with a 15-day refund.

The honest trade-off: you give up the strongest cloud models. GreenCube's AI is smaller than GPT or Claude and weaker on the hardest problems, and speed depends on your PC (on the PCs we tested, 5 to 7 tokens a second on an 8 GB laptop, 14 to 18 on a 16 GB one). It's also built for one person on one PC, not shared team workspaces, so a team is better served by LibreChat or a managed platform.

How to choose

  1. Work out roughly how much you use AI each month, and what that costs you now.
  2. Decide how sensitive your data is. Client files and medical notes point to local or self-hosted.
  3. Check whether you need shared history and admin controls for a team, or it's just you.
  4. Be honest about setup time. A self-hosted server is free to license but not free to run.

Questions

How much does TypingMind cost?

TypingMind sells a licence for its interface, and on top of that you pay your AI provider (OpenAI, Anthropic and others) per token with your own API key. So the total depends on how much you use it; check its site for the current licence price.

What is the best frontend for LLMs?

There's no single best one. LibreChat and Open WebUI suit people who want an open-source interface on their own server, Poe suits people who want many models with no API keys, and local apps like Jan or GreenCube run the AI on your own computer.

What AI is replacing ChatGPT for privacy-focused users?

Nothing fully replaces ChatGPT's strongest models. Privacy-focused users often move to local apps such as Jan or GreenCube, which run a smaller model on their own computer so messages don't go to an AI company, accepting weaker answers on the hardest problems.

What is the best open-source chat UI for LLMs?

LibreChat is the open-source, self-hosted chat UI most people land on, and Open WebUI is popular with developers who mix local and API models. Both need someone to install, patch and back them up.

Is GreenCube a good TypingMind alternative for teams?

Not really. GreenCube is built for one person on one Windows PC, not shared team workspaces, so a team is better served by LibreChat or a managed platform like Poe or Aymo AI.
