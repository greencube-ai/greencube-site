Gemini Alternatives for Private, Offline Windows AI

By Hector Gras · 17 August 2026

We checked this
  • Which AI doesn't train on your chats? We checked 9 (October 2026)

If you want a Gemini alternative that runs on your own Windows PC and keeps your chats private, the main options are GreenCube, Ollama, Foundry Local, OnPrem.LLM and llama.cpp. GreenCube is the easiest for non-technical users (€9.99 once, nothing to configure); the other four are free and give more control, but expect some setup, and two of them are really tools for developers.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. None of these is as capable as Gemini, which runs on Google's servers and uses Google Search. What they offer instead is an AI that works without internet and never sends your files anywhere.

GreenCube

GreenCube is for people who want a private AI without learning how local AI works. You install it, it checks your PC and downloads the model that fits, and then it works offline. Fittingly for a Gemini alternative, its models are built on Gemma, Google's open model family: Cube 2B on smaller PCs and Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card.

It chats, reads PDFs, Word files and photos, and makes slide decks that save as PowerPoint, quizzes, flashcards, labelled diagrams and pictures. Your chats and files are saved on your PC. It costs €9.99 (US$9.99) once with no message limits. The catches: Windows only, a one-time sign-in so it can check you paid, no API for other software, and on a slow PC it's slow. On the PCs we tested it wrote 5 to 7 tokens a second on an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card (about reading speed), 14 to 18 on a 16 GB laptop and 37 to 76 on most gaming PCs with a 6 GB or bigger card.

Ollama

Ollama is one of the most popular free ways to run open models on your own computer. You pull a model with one command, or from the chat window it added for Windows and Mac in 2025, and other apps can connect to it through its local API. It suits people who like trying different models and don't mind choosing sizes themselves. More in Ollama vs GreenCube.

Foundry Local

Foundry Local is Microsoft's runtime for running AI models on the device. It picks a version of each model that suits your hardware (graphics card, NPU or processor), speaks the same API format as OpenAI, and comes with SDKs for several programming languages. There are no per-use costs and no Azure subscription needed. It's built for developers who want to put local AI inside their own apps; it isn't a chat app for everyday use.

OnPrem.LLM

OnPrem.LLM is an open-source Python toolkit for using AI on sensitive documents: summarising, extracting information and answering questions about a collection of files. It runs on llama.cpp by default and can also use Ollama and other backends, and it includes a simple web app. It suits technical teams who need private document search; you'll need to be comfortable with Python to set it up.

llama.cpp

llama.cpp is the open-source engine underneath a lot of local AI. It runs compressed models efficiently on ordinary processors and graphics cards. It gives you the most control of anything here, and asks the most of you: you work from the command line and pick every setting yourself.

What you give up compared with Gemini

A model that fits on a laptop is smaller than Gemini, and it shows on the hardest problems and on very long documents. Without the web, it also doesn't know today's news. Gemini also lives inside Gmail, Docs and Android, which none of these do. In exchange you get an AI that works offline, keeps your files on your PC and has no monthly fee. Questions

What is the best Gemini alternative for offline Windows use?

GreenCube is the simplest if you're not technical: you install it and it picks the model for your PC. Ollama suits people who like choosing models, and Foundry Local, OnPrem.LLM and llama.cpp are mainly for developers.

Do these tools require any internet connection at all?

Only at the start: once a model is downloaded, all of them can chat offline. GreenCube needs internet once to sign in with Google or Microsoft (just to check you paid) and to download the AI, and after that only for updates or if you switch Online on.

How much storage do local AI models need?

It depends on the model: small ones made for laptops take a few gigabytes each, bigger ones much more. Check the download size before you pick.

Can these apps read PDFs and images?

GreenCube reads PDFs, Word files and photos out of the box. With the developer tools it depends on the model you choose and how you set it up; OnPrem.LLM is built for document work.

Is local AI as capable as cloud models like Gemini?

No. Gemini is much bigger and uses Google Search, so it's better at the hardest problems and knows today's news. Local models give you offline use, privacy and no monthly fee instead.
