Verify Private Document AI Locally: Checklist for Professionals

By Hector Gras · 7 October 2026

Private document AI is AI that reads, searches and summarises your files while the content stays under your control. There are three ways to get it: an app that runs entirely on your own computer, a hosted workspace where the provider promises in a contract not to use or share your data, or a masking layer that strips names and numbers before text goes to a cloud AI. Whichever you choose, don't take "private" on trust. The six-step check below shows you where your files actually go.

I build GreenCube, which is the first kind, so weigh my view with that in mind. The checklist works on any tool, mine included.

What "private" actually means

"Private" isn't one thing. It describes where your files, the text pulled out of them and the AI's working copy physically sit, and who can see them along the way.

A local app does everything on your machine. A hosted private workspace runs on the vendor's servers but adds guarantees such as encryption, separation from other customers and a promise not to train on your data. Microsoft's description of how its enterprise AI service handles data is a good example of that kind of promise. A masking layer sits in front of a normal cloud AI and swaps sensitive details for placeholders before anything is sent.

Whatever the label, a document can leak at several points:

  • the file itself, when it's uploaded or imported
  • the text read from a scan or photo, if that reading happens on someone else's server
  • any search index built from your documents, wherever it's stored
  • your questions and the answers, plus logs and crash reports
  • backups, which can keep copies long after you're done

What to ask any vendor

  1. Are files encrypted in transit and at rest, and in which country are they stored?
  2. For hosted tools, is my data kept separate from other customers'?
  3. How long are chats, uploads and backups kept, and can I delete them myself?
  4. Is "we don't train on your data" in the contract, and what do logs and telemetry still collect?
  5. Who in my team can use it, and is there an audit trail?

Ask for the boundary in writing. "Never sent anywhere", "not used for training" and "encrypted" are three different promises, and "we respect your privacy" is none of them.

A six-step check you can run today

Claims are easy to make and quick to test. Before you rely on a tool for real work:

  1. Import a PDF or scan that looks like what you actually handle.
  2. Find out whether reading the scan happens on your device or gets sent somewhere.
  3. Disconnect from the internet and see whether it still works.
  4. Watch outgoing network connections while you do a normal task.
  5. Find where chats, indexes, crash reports and backups are stored.
  6. Take a quote or page reference from an answer and check it against the original, line by line.

Fitting it into how you already work

You don't need to replace your document system. Keep files where they already live, a shared drive or case management tool, and use the AI as a separate place to read and draft: open the specific files you need, work on them, and save the result back yourself. That avoids giving a new tool automatic access to everything you store.

Decide which kinds of documents may go into any AI tool before someone decides informally. For teams, keep track of which machines hold sensitive files, and remember that with a local app the security of that laptop is now entirely yours.

Local, hosted or masking: which fits?

A local app gives the strongest boundary, since your files stay on the machine, but it's limited by the machine and you're responsible for securing it. A hosted private workspace gives up some of that boundary for central access control, audit logs and contracts, which larger organisations often need. Masking is a practical middle path when a local model can't handle the job, but only if you've checked that the masking really catches what it should.

Questions

Is there an AI that is completely private?

Nearly: an app that runs on your own computer and sends nothing out, on a machine you've secured properly. How private any AI is depends on where it runs and how it's set up.

Is it legal to use AI to write legal documents?

Generally yes, but rules on review, disclosure and liability vary by country and area of law. Have a qualified lawyer check anything AI-assisted before it's filed or relied on, and check whether confidentiality rules limit which tools you can use with client information.

What is the best AI for documents?

It depends on what you need. Cloud tools handle complex material best, hosted business tools add collaboration and contracts, and local apps keep files on your own machine, so pick the trade-off that fits your documents.

Can I make a private AI?

Yes. Free apps like LM Studio, Jan and Ollama run open models on your own computer if you're happy choosing one, and paid apps like GreenCube set it up and choose for you. Either way, it runs offline after the first download.
