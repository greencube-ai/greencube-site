Claude vs GreenCube: How Local and Cloud AI Actually Differ

The core difference isn't features. It's where the thinking happens.

Claude runs on Anthropic's servers. Every message you send travels to a data center, gets processed by a large model, and comes back over the internet. That's how it can offer models like Claude Fable 5.1 and Opus 5, with context windows up to 1 million tokens, and access to huge compute clusters for heavy reasoning. GreenCube works the opposite way: the model lives on your Windows or Mac computer, and inference happens on your own processor and memory, with nothing sent to a remote server for the chat itself.

That architectural split shows up in real, practical ways:

  • Latency: cloud responses depend on your internet connection and Anthropic's server load; local responses depend only on your CPU and RAM.
  • Model size: cloud providers can run models far larger than any laptop could hold, which is why Claude tends to outperform smaller local models on complex reasoning and coding benchmarks.
  • Updates: Claude's models update automatically on Anthropic's schedule; GreenCube's models update only when you choose to download a new version.
  • Offline access: Claude requires an active connection every time; GreenCube keeps working on a plane, in a basement office, or during an internet outage.
  • Image and tool support: Claude includes web search and broad tool integrations out of the box; GreenCube's image reading and document tools depend on which local model you pick.

None of this makes one architecture universally "better." It just means each one solves a different problem. A local versus cloud AI primer is worth a read if you want the fuller technical picture before deciding.

What Data Actually Leaves Your Device?

This is the question most comparisons skip, and it's the one that actually matters for anyone handling client contracts, medical notes, or an unpublished manuscript.

With GreenCube, the only thing that touches the internet is a one-time sign-in through Google or Microsoft, used purely to verify your license. After that, your chats, documents, and images stay on your machine with no telemetry sent back. Any online feature only activates if you switch it on yourself.

Claude works differently by design. Every prompt, document upload, and follow-up question gets processed on Anthropic's servers, which is what lets it draw on massive models and maintain features like persistent memory across sessions. Anthropic offers enterprise-grade controls for business accounts, but the data still leaves your device to get processed, and that's an exposure point some professions simply can't accept for sensitive material.

A few practical habits protect you regardless of which tool you use:

  • Turn on full-disk encryption (BitLocker on Windows, FileVault on Mac) so local files stay protected if a device is lost or stolen.
  • Keep a manual backup routine for anything GreenCube helps you draft, since local ownership means you're responsible for your own copies.
  • Think before pasting cloud-generated output into a document containing information you wouldn't want indexed somewhere else.

Pro Tip: If you're handling anything under attorney-client privilege, HR records, or unreleased creative work, treat "the data never leaves my computer" as a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have. That single design choice eliminates an entire category of risk that no amount of enterprise policy on a cloud platform can fully close.

Which GreenCube Model Fits Your Hardware?

GreenCube gives you two models at setup, and picking the right one matters more than most people expect.

  1. Quick (Llama 3.2 3B, about 2GB) handles plain text fast, on almost any modern laptop, but can't read images or PDFs. Pick it if you mostly want drafting, brainstorming, or quick answers with no visual documents involved.
  2. All-rounder (Gemma 4 E4B, about 4.2GB) reads images and builds study guides or documents, but runs slower and needs at least 8GB of RAM to feel responsive. Pick it if your work involves scanned PDFs, screenshots, or building structured documents from source material.

Hardware reality matters here. A machine with 8GB of RAM and an older CPU will run All-rounder, but expect noticeably longer pauses on long documents or multi-image sessions. A machine with 16GB and a recent processor handles both models comfortably, including longer sessions without slowdown. If you're on a five-year-old budget laptop, Quick is the safer starting point, and you can always switch models later if you need image support.

Session length matters too. Short back-and-forth chats feel snappy on almost any setup. Long sessions with big documents loaded into context will slow down on weaker hardware regardless of which model you pick. If speed is your top priority and you rarely touch images, Quick is the better everyday choice, even on a strong machine.

Claude works on a subscription model, and Anthropic's own pricing and plans page shows how costs scale with usage: free access covers light use, while paid tiers unlock higher limits, Claude Code, and research features. The gap between a one-time license and a year of subscription fees is the single clearest number in this whole comparison, and it only widens the longer you keep using the tool.

How to Choose: A Quick Checklist Before You Decide

Run through this before picking a side.

  1. How often do you actually use AI? Daily, heavy use for coding or research points toward cloud-first tools built for scale. Occasional drafting, note-taking, or document review points toward a one-time local tool.
  2. How sensitive is your data? If you're handling client files, medical information, or anything you'd rather never touch a third-party server, that alone should settle the decision toward local.
  3. What's your hardware? Check your RAM before committing. Under 8GB, stick with lightweight local models or lean cloud; 8GB or more opens up richer local options.
  4. Do you need collaboration or team tools? Multi-user workflows and shared cloud documents favor Claude's account-based structure over a single-device local installation.

Watch for red flags on either side: a cloud tool with a vague data-retention policy, or a local tool that promises privacy but quietly phones home. GreenCube's answer to that last one is architectural, not a policy promise: without an internet connection, there's simply nothing to send.

Pro Tip: If you're not sure how often you'll actually use AI, that uncertainty itself is useful information. Light or unpredictable use almost always favors a flat one-time cost over a recurring bill you might forget to cancel.
