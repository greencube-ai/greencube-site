Notion AI Alternatives: Pay Once for a Private Offline Assistant

By Hector Gras · 15 October 2026

We checked this
  • AI without a subscription: what actually exists in 2026, with checked prices

The right Notion AI alternative depends on what you used Notion AI for. For team docs and tables, Coda is the closest swap. For notes that organise themselves, Mem. For linked notes kept as files on your own computer, Obsidian. For research answers grounded in your own documents, NotebookLM. And if your notes and files are sensitive, GreenCube runs the AI on your PC, works offline, and costs €9.99 once.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Below is each real option, who it suits, and the catch. Most of them run in the cloud, which is fine for many people; I've noted where it isn't.

Workspaces and note apps

Coda mixes documents and tables, and its AI writes into both. It's built for repeating team work like trackers and planning docs, and it's the most direct competitor to Notion for that. Cloud-based.

Mem is for people who want to dump notes in without filing them. Its AI sorts what you capture and brings older ideas back when they're relevant. It has less structure than a full workspace. Cloud-based.

Obsidian keeps your notes as plain files on your own computer and links them into a graph. The core app is free. AI comes through community plugins, and whether a plugin runs locally or calls a cloud service depends on which one you pick, so check before you install. Setup takes more effort, and it rewards people who like to tinker.

Tana organises everything as linked, structured items using what it calls supertags. It's powerful once you learn it, and steep for beginners. Cloud-based.

Taskade puts notes, task lists and simple AI helpers in one shared workspace, for small teams that want a single light tool. Cloud-based.

AI that acts or answers

Saner.AI is built around agents that work on your notes and tasks across connected apps. It's the closest match to the agent side of Notion AI. Cloud only.

NotebookLM answers questions from the documents you upload and points you back to the source. It's strong for study and research, but it isn't a place for daily notes. Cloud-based (NotebookLM alternatives).

An assistant that stays on your computer

GreenCube is the one I make. It's a Windows app that runs the AI on your own PC, so your chats and files are saved there and never sent anywhere. It picks the model for your computer (Cube 2B on smaller PCs, Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma). You can paste in notes or open PDFs, Word files and photos, and ask it to summarise, answer questions, or turn them into a document, flashcards, a quiz or a slide deck.

You sign in once with Google or Microsoft, only to check you paid, and the first launch downloads the model. After that it works offline. It costs €9.99 once, with no subscription and a 15-day refund.

What it isn't: a notes workspace. It won't hold your pages and databases like Notion, and it doesn't run agents across your other apps. Its AI is smaller than the big cloud models, so it's weaker on the hardest reasoning, and on a slow PC it's slow (on the PCs we tested, 5 to 7 tokens a second on an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card, 14 to 18 on a 16 GB one). If you want both, Obsidian and GreenCube can sit side by side: notes as files on your PC, and an AI on the same PC you can paste them into.

How to choose

  1. Decide how sensitive your content is. Client files, medical notes or unpublished work point to a tool that keeps them on your machine.
  2. Decide whether you need a workspace, an assistant, or both.
  3. If you want agents that act across other apps, a cloud tool will fit better than a local one.
  4. Compare the cost over a full year, not the first month.
  5. Try one real task, like summarising a document you actually use, before you commit.

During any trial it's worth asking where your data is stored, whether it's used to train models, and whether anything works with the internet off. For more on that, see AI that doesn't train on your data and the best AI desktop apps.

Questions

Is there a free alternative to Notion AI?

Yes. Obsidian's core app is free, and NotebookLM and Taskade have free plans, though free versions usually limit use or features. Cloud tools send your content to a server, so if that matters, a local app is the other way to go.

Is Notion still relevant in 2026?

Yes, it's still widely used for shared workspaces and databases. Its AI features are one reason people stay and also one reason some leave, over cost and data worries.

What are some good alternatives to Notion without AI?

Obsidian is a strong pick for linked, local notes, with AI only if you add plugins. Taskade and Coda also work as plain notes and task tools if you ignore the AI features.

Who is Notion's biggest competitor?

It depends on the job: Coda on documents and tables, Obsidian on personal knowledge, and Taskade on light team notes and tasks.
