Keep Gemini Local? Run Gemma Offline with GreenCube on Windows

By Hector Gras · 20 September 2026 · updated 10 October 2026

You can't run Google's Gemini on your own PC: it lives on Google's servers and needs internet. What you can keep local is Gemma, Google's open model family built from the same research as Gemini. GreenCube is a Windows app that installs a Gemma-based model for you and runs it offline, with your chats and files kept on your computer, for €9.99 (US$9.99) once.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. This guide is for people who like Gemini but want an AI that stays on their own machine, without learning anything technical.

Can Gemini run offline?

Not the Gemini you chat with. The Gemini website and apps send your messages to Google, where a very large model writes the answer, so with no connection there's nothing to answer you. Google does run a small version called Gemini Nano on some Android phones and inside Chrome, but that powers specific built-in features rather than a chat you can use like the Gemini app.

Gemma is different. Google releases these models openly, so anyone can download them and run them on their own computer. That's the practical way to "keep Gemini local": a smaller relative of it that never sends anything to Google.

How GreenCube runs Gemma for you

Running Gemma yourself usually means picking a model file, a size and an app to run it in. GreenCube does those choices for you. It checks your PC and installs Cube 2B (built on Gemma 4 E2B) on smaller PCs, or Cube 4B (built on Gemma 4 E4B) on PCs with a graphics card. If you're curious about the difference, Gemma 4 E2B vs E4B explains it.

Besides chatting, it makes slide decks that save as PowerPoint, documents, quizzes, flashcards, labelled diagrams and pictures, and it reads PDFs, Word files and photos. The app is in 19 languages.

Setting it up

  1. Download GreenCube and install it on Windows 10 or 11.
  2. Sign in once with Google or Microsoft. This only checks that you paid; it never sees your chats or files.
  3. Let it check your PC and download the model on first launch. This is the one big download, so do it on a decent connection.
  4. Switch the Wi-Fi off and try a chat. From now on it works offline, and only goes online for updates or if you turn on Online for web lookups.

Keeping it private

With GreenCube, your chats, files and images are saved on your PC, so the privacy of that data now depends on your PC. A few ordinary Windows habits close the gaps:

  • Use a password or PIN and lock the screen when you step away.
  • Turn on device encryption or BitLocker, so a lost laptop doesn't expose your files.
  • Check what OneDrive or other backup tools sync. If they copy your whole user folder to the cloud, files you exported from the app go with it.
  • Keep Windows up to date.

If something goes wrong

If the first download is slow or stops, check your connection and whether antivirus or a work firewall is blocking large downloads, then try again. If answers feel slow, close heavy programs like a browser with dozens of tabs, and remember that an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card writes at about reading speed; that's normal for the hardware, not a fault. Pictures need 12 GB of memory, so a PC with less can't make them.

Questions

Can I run Google's Gemini on my own PC?

No. The Gemini you chat with runs on Google's servers and needs the internet. What you can run locally is Gemma, Google's open model family built from the same research, and GreenCube's Cube models are built on it.

How Much Does GreenCube Cost?

Can GreenCube Read PDFs and Images?

Yes. It reads PDFs, Word files and photos on any PC it runs on, from about 8 GB of memory up. Making pictures, rather than reading them, needs 12 GB.

Does GreenCube Need the Internet to Work?

Only at the start: it needs the internet once, to sign in with Google or Microsoft (only to check you paid) and to download the AI. After that it works offline, and goes online only for updates or if you switch Online on for web lookups.

Is Local AI Actually More Private Than Cloud AI?

Your chats and files stay on your PC instead of going to a company's servers, which removes the biggest privacy risk. Their privacy then depends on your PC, so use a password, turn on device encryption and check what OneDrive or other backups sync.
