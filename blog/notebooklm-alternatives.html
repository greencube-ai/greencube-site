NotebookLM alternatives for studying: free, open-source, offline (2026)

By Hector Gras · 18 August 2026 · updated 23 October 2026

Short answer: NotebookLM is hard to beat for free: up to 50 sources per notebook, answers that cite your notes, and no training on your uploads unless you send feedback. Look elsewhere if you hit its limits, if you don't want your notes on Google's servers, or if you study offline. Then: an AI on your own PC (GreenCube, or the free AnythingLLM), the open-source Open Notebook, or page-citing PDF tools like Humata and Adobe Acrobat's AI Assistant.

What NotebookLM does well, and why people switch

NotebookLM (now also called Gemini Notebook) answers from the sources you give it, cites the part each answer comes from, and makes flashcards and quizzes you can export as CSV (Google). Google says it doesn't train on your uploads unless you rate an answer (Google). The usual reasons to look elsewhere:

  • Your notes go to Google. Some students, and some schools' or academies' materials, shouldn't be uploaded.
  • It needs internet and a Google account.
  • Limits: 50 sources per notebook and 100 notebooks on the free plan (Google).

Pick by how you study

You need citations for essays

Stay on NotebookLM if uploading is fine, or use Humata or Acrobat's AI Assistant, which also point back to the page. Check every quote against the PDF anyway.

Your notes shouldn't leave your PC

An AI on your own computer reads files from your disk. GreenCube (Windows, €9.99 once) picks the AI for your PC and also makes study guides and flashcards; AnythingLLM is free if you're happy choosing a model.

You want NotebookLM, but open source

Open Notebook (MIT) is the closest in shape: notebooks of sources, chat over them, even podcast-style audio, on your own server, with cloud or local models.

Questions

What is the best NotebookLM alternative for studying?

If you want answers that cite your sources, Humata and Adobe Acrobat's AI Assistant also link back to the page, but both cap the free tier or need a paid plan. If you don't want your notes on Google's servers, an AI on your own PC reads them from your disk: GreenCube (€9.99 once, Windows) or the free AnythingLLM. Open Notebook is an open-source, self-hosted NotebookLM-style app.

Is there a free NotebookLM alternative?

Yes. AnythingLLM's desktop app and Open Notebook are free and open source (MIT); Humata gives 60 pages a month free and ChatPDF 2 documents a day. NotebookLM itself is also free, with up to 50 sources per notebook.

Is there an open-source NotebookLM alternative?

Open Notebook (MIT) is a self-hosted, NotebookLM-style app that handles PDFs, audio, video and web pages and can use local models through Ollama or LM Studio. AnythingLLM (MIT) is a simpler desktop app for chatting with your documents.

Is there a NotebookLM alternative that works offline?

Yes: an AI that runs on your own computer. GreenCube and AnythingLLM's desktop app read your PDFs on your PC with no upload. In our test, GreenCube answered 11 of 12 questions about long documents right, about 15 seconds each, on a 16 GB laptop.

Does NotebookLM train on my notes?

Google says NotebookLM doesn't train on your uploads unless you send feedback (thumbs up or down). Your notes are still stored on Google's servers.
