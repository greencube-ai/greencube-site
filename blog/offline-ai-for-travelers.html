Offline AI for Travelers: The Private, No-Cloud Guide

By Hector Gras · 30 July 2026

An offline AI is one that runs on your own laptop, so it keeps working on a plane, in a remote area or with dead hotel Wi-Fi. You download it at home, test it with the Wi-Fi off, and then use it anywhere for writing, planning, reading documents and explaining things. What it can't do offline is anything live: flight changes, maps, opening hours, exchange rates and news still need a connection.

I build GreenCube, an offline AI app for Windows, so take my opinion with that in mind. Most of this guide applies whichever app you use.

What "offline" means for a traveller

Apps like ChatGPT send every message to a server, so they stop working the moment you lose signal, and on roaming data every message costs something. An offline AI keeps the model on your laptop's disk and does the work on your own processor. Once it's installed, airplane mode makes no difference to it.

There's a privacy side too. When you ask about your itinerary, paste in a booking or show it a photo of your documents, all of that stays on your laptop instead of passing through someone else's server.

What it's good for on the road

  • Writing: emails to a hotel, a complaint to an airline, a message in another language.
  • Replanning: "my train now arrives at 4pm, rework tomorrow around that".
  • Reading documents: summarise a long rental contract or insurance PDF, or find the one line about cancellations.
  • Photos: show it a menu, a sign or a form and ask what it says. Check anything important, because a small model can misread.
  • Explaining: local customs, how a ticket system works, what a word means.
  • Killing time: notes, journaling, or studying on a long flight.

What it can't do

An offline model only knows what it learned before it was released. It doesn't know about today's strike, your gate change, the weather or whether a museum is open. It can't book anything or show you a map. For those, you still need the internet or an app built for that job. For offline maps and translation on your phone, Google Translate and Microsoft Translator both let you download languages ahead of time, and Google Maps lets you save areas for offline use.

It's also less clever than the big cloud AIs on the hardest questions. For travel tasks that rarely matters, but it's worth knowing.

Before you go

  1. Install the app and finish the first-run setup at home, on good Wi-Fi. The AI itself is a large download, and airport or hotel Wi-Fi is the worst place to try it.
  2. Sign in if the app needs it, and install any pending updates.
  3. Switch on airplane mode and ask it a question. Then give it a PDF and a photo. If all three work, you're ready.
  4. Copy the documents you might need (bookings, insurance, tickets) to the laptop so you can open them, and ask about them, with no signal.
  5. Turn on disk encryption (BitLocker or Device Encryption on Windows) and set a password, since your documents are on a laptop that could be lost.
  6. Pack the charger. Running an AI works the processor hard, so the battery drains faster than when you're browsing.

What your laptop needs

A local AI is heavier than a normal app. On the PCs we tested, GreenCube writes 5–7 tokens a second on an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card, which is about reading speed, and 14–18 on a 16 GB laptop. A thin travel laptop with 8 GB works, just more slowly. Leave some free disk space for the model, and check your machine with Can my PC run AI? before you rely on it. If your laptop has no graphics card, AI on a laptop without a GPU explains what to expect.

Paying for it

Some offline AI apps are free, like LM Studio, Jan and Ollama, where you choose and download a model yourself. Others charge once. Subscriptions are the awkward option for travel: a card that expires or gets blocked abroad can cut off access when you least want it. With a one-time purchase there's nothing to renew mid-trip. Questions

Does offline AI work with no Wi-Fi at all?

Yes, once it's installed and the AI is downloaded. Set it up at home, then test it in airplane mode. Anything live, like flight changes, maps or news, still needs a connection.

How large are the model files I need to download first?

Usually a few gigabytes, so download them at home on good Wi-Fi. Airport and hotel Wi-Fi is the worst place to try it.

Is my data private if I use GreenCube?

Your chats, files and photos are processed and saved on your laptop. GreenCube goes online to sign in (only to check you paid), to download the AI once, for updates, and for web lookups only if you switch Online on.

How do I protect the travel documents on my laptop?

Turn on disk encryption (BitLocker or Device Encryption on Windows) and set a password, since a laptop can be lost. GreenCube doesn't encrypt your disk itself; that's Windows' job.

What happens when GreenCube releases an update?

When you're online, GreenCube checks for a newer version, and every update is signed and checked by the app before it installs. Install any pending update at home before a trip.
