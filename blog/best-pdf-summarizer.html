The Best PDF Summarizer for People Who Won't Upload Sensitive Files

If you won't upload a sensitive PDF, the best summarizer is one that runs on your own computer, so the file stays on it. Free apps like GPT4All and AnythingLLM can do this if you're happy to set them up; GreenCube does it with no setup for €9.99 once on Windows. If the document isn't sensitive, the cloud tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, NotebookLM, Adobe Acrobat's AI Assistant) are more capable, especially on long or difficult documents.

I make GreenCube, so read my opinion with that in mind. Below is how to choose, the options on each side, and how to get a summary you can trust from any of them.

What matters when you choose

The first question isn't speed or price. It's where the processing happens. A cloud summarizer sends your file to a server, runs it through a big model and sends back the result: stronger models, nothing to install, but the document leaves your machine and you need a connection every time. A local summarizer keeps the file and the model on your computer, so nothing is uploaded and it works on a plane.

After that, check four things:

  • Can it point you back to the page a claim came from, so you can check it?
  • Does it read charts, tables and scanned pages, or only the text layer?
  • How long a document can it handle in one go?
  • Is it free with caps, a subscription, or a one-time price?

Cloud summarizers

ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all accept PDF uploads and summarise them well, and Google's NotebookLM is built around asking questions of your sources. Adobe's AI Assistant works inside Acrobat; Adobe lists a limit of about 600 pages and 100 MB per file, which covers most single documents. There are also dedicated PDF chat sites, compared in Humata alternatives and PDF.ai alternatives.

They're the right choice for public or low-stakes material, or when you need the strongest model for a dense paper. The catch is the same for all of them: the file is on someone else's server, under their retention and training rules, and free tiers cap how much you can do. For what ChatGPT keeps, see is ChatGPT private?

Local summarizers

GPT4All and AnythingLLM are free apps that let you chat with your own documents on your computer. You pick and download a model yourself, which is fine if you enjoy that and a hurdle if you've never heard of a GGUF file. There's a wider comparison in private PDF AI.

The trade-off with any local tool: the model is smaller than the cloud ones. How to get a summary you can trust

  1. Prepare the file. If it's a scan, make sure it's legible, and cut pages you don't need, like appendices or covers.
  2. Decide the format first: bullet points for a skim, a short summary for someone who won't read the original, or section by section for something you'll come back to.
  3. Be specific. Ask for a set number of points, the page for each claim, and anything that looks like a number, date or obligation flagged.
  4. Check it. Open the pages it cites and read them. AI summaries can sound sure and still be wrong, especially on numbers and names.
  5. Keep the summary next to the original, so you can find the source again later.

Treat the first summary as a draft and use follow-up questions to get what it missed.

Questions

Can ChatGPT summarize a PDF?

Yes, if you upload it or paste the text. It runs in the cloud, so the document goes to OpenAI's servers and you need an internet connection.

Is there a free AI PDF summarizer available?

Yes. The big chatbots have free tiers with usage caps, and GPT4All and AnythingLLM are free apps that summarise on your own computer once you've set up a model.

Which free AI summarizer is the best?

For public documents, a free tier of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or NotebookLM. For sensitive ones, a free local app if you don't mind the setup, since nothing is uploaded.

Which AI tool is best for PDF analysis?

Cloud tools are strongest on long, complex documents that are fine to share. For contracts, medical records or unpublished work that shouldn't leave your machine, use a local tool.

Does a local PDF summarizer handle images and figures?

Some do. It depends on whether the model can read images. GreenCube's models can read photos and pages with figures, but check any numbers it reads from a chart against the original.
