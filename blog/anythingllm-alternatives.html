AnythingLLM Alternatives for Private, Offline AI in 2026

By Hector Gras · 18 October 2026

The best AnythingLLM alternative depends on what put you off. If you want more control and don't mind tinkering, look at LM Studio, Jan, Ollama or Open WebUI. If you want the simplest private app for asking questions about your PDFs and photos, with nothing to configure and one price, GreenCube is the one I built for that. All of them can run with the internet off once a model is downloaded.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. AnythingLLM is a good tool: it's open source (MIT licensed, made by Mintplex Labs, as DataCamp's guide notes) and it does a lot. That's also why people leave it.

Why people look beyond AnythingLLM

AnythingLLM is a workspace for chatting with your documents. To get going you pick a model provider, then there are embedders, vector databases and workspaces to understand. For someone who just wants to ask a question about a PDF, that's a lot on day one. Others leave because they want something different: help with code, a shared server for a team, or a plain chat window without the document machinery.

LM Studio

A desktop app for downloading and running many different models, with a chat window and basic document chat. It suits people who like comparing models and changing settings. The trade-off is that you choose the model, the size and the settings yourself, which is fun if you enjoy it and a chore if you don't. The app itself isn't open source, so for what it sends you rely on its privacy policy. More in LM Studio alternatives.

Jan

A free, open-source chat app that runs models on your machine. Because the code is public, anyone can check what leaves your computer, which is a real advantage over closed apps. It's friendlier than a terminal tool, but it's more of a chat window than a document tool, so check how far its file features go today if PDFs are your main job.

Ollama

Ollama is the engine, not the car. It downloads and serves models, and many other apps plug into it. On its own it doesn't give you document chat: something on top has to split your files, index them and feed the right parts to the model, which is the part AnythingLLM does. Great for developers; if you're not comfortable in a terminal, Ollama alone probably isn't your answer. See Ollama vs LM Studio.

Open WebUI

A polished, ChatGPT-style web interface for models you host yourself. It shines as a shared service on a spare machine for a small office or a tech-minded household. For one person on one laptop it's more than you need, and you should expect to deal with Docker or a server. Options in Open WebUI alternatives.

For coding: Aider or Cline

If what you want is help with code, a document app is the wrong category. Aider (in the terminal) and Cline (in VS Code) are coding assistants that can work with local models. Pick them for programming, not for studying a PDF.

GreenCube

GreenCube is for people who want the result without the setup. You install it on Windows, sign in once with Google or Microsoft (only to check you paid), and on first launch it checks your PC and downloads the model that fits: Cube 2B on smaller laptops, Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma. Then you drop in a PDF, a Word file or a photo and ask about it, or turn it into a summary, flashcards, a quiz or slides. Chats and files stay on your computer, and after setup it works offline. It goes online only for updates, or if you switch on web lookups.

It costs €9.99 once, with a 15-day refund. What it doesn't do: it isn't as smart as the big cloud AIs on hard problems, it has none of AnythingLLM's knobs, and there's no Mac or Linux version. It also holds no compliance certification; what it offers instead is that you can turn the Wi-Fi off and watch it keep working.

How to tell if a "private" app really is private

Don't take any label on trust, including mine.

  • Check that the chat model runs on your machine. Many apps switch between local and cloud models, so look at the active setting.
  • Check where documents are indexed. If the step that turns your files into searchable pieces uses a cloud service, your document text leaves the computer even when the chat feels local.
  • Ask what else it sends: licence checks, telemetry, updates. A privacy policy should say.
  • Turn the Wi-Fi off and try a chat and a PDF. It's the simplest test there is.

There's a longer checklist in private document AI.

What your computer can handle

Local AI needs memory. On a laptop with 8 GB of RAM it works but it's slow, and it gets slower if a browser full of tabs is fighting it for memory. On the PCs we tested, GreenCube writes 5 to 7 tokens a second on an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card (about reading speed), 14 to 18 on a 16 GB laptop, and 37 to 76 on most gaming PCs with a 6 GB or bigger card. Other apps running similar-sized models land in the same range. Close heavy programs before a long document chat, and check yours with Can my PC run AI?

Questions

Is AnythingLLM free?

The open-source desktop app is free, and paid cloud options exist; check its site for current pricing. You still need a model behind it, local or through an API.

Do I need technical skills to run a local AI app?

Not for every tool. Ollama and Open WebUI expect some comfort with a terminal or containers. LM Studio and Jan are desktop apps but ask you to choose models, and GreenCube installs like any program and picks the model for you.

Does GreenCube send my documents anywhere?

No. Chats and files stay on your computer. The sign-in only checks that you paid and never sends a chat or a file, and it goes online only for updates or if you switch on web lookups.

Will it run on my old laptop?

If it has about 8 GB of RAM, yes, everything works, just slowly: about reading speed on the laptops we tested. With 16 GB it's two to three times faster.

Can I use GreenCube on Linux?

No. GreenCube is for Windows 10 and 11 only.
