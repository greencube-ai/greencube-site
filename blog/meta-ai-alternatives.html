$9.99 Privacy First Meta AI Alternatives for Windows

By Hector Gras · 29 September 2026

We checked this
  • Which AI doesn't train on your chats? We checked 9 (October 2026)

If you want a Meta AI alternative that's simply another assistant, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Microsoft Copilot all do the same job in the cloud. If what bothers you is that your chats go through Meta's servers and your Meta account, the real alternative is an AI that runs on your own computer, such as the free apps LM Studio or Ollama, or GreenCube for €9.99 once. Those keep your chats on your PC and work offline.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Here's how the two kinds of alternative compare, and what you give up by leaving the cloud.

What Meta AI is, and why people look elsewhere

Meta AI is the assistant built into WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook and Messenger, and it also has its own website. It runs on Meta's own Llama models, on Meta's servers. That makes it handy if you already live in those apps, but it also means every question goes to Meta and is tied to your account, and nothing works without a connection.

Those are the two reasons people usually go looking: they want an assistant outside Meta's apps, or they want one that doesn't send their conversations anywhere at all.

Cloud alternatives

If you're happy with the cloud and just want a different company or a stronger model, these are the big ones. All of them have free plans and paid plans, and all of them keep your chats on their servers.

  • ChatGPT from OpenAI, the most widely used general assistant.
  • Gemini from Google, which fits well if you use Gmail and Google Docs.
  • Claude from Anthropic, good for long writing and documents.
  • Microsoft Copilot, built into Windows and Microsoft 365.

Any of these will out-think a model running on a laptop on hard problems. None of them solves the privacy question; they move it to a different company.

Offline alternatives

An offline AI is a model file on your own disk plus an app to talk to it. You download the model once, and from then on you can switch the Wi-Fi off. They come in roughly three kinds.

Ready-to-use apps

You install one program and start typing. GreenCube is one: it checks your PC and picks the model that fits (Cube 2B on smaller PCs, Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma). Besides chat it makes slide decks, documents, flashcards, quizzes and images, and reads your PDFs and photos. It's for Windows and costs €9.99 once, with a 15-day refund.

Free do-it-yourself apps

LM Studio, Ollama, Jan and GPT4All are free. You choose a model from a long list, download it and chat. One nice detail: Meta publishes its Llama models for anyone to download, so you can run a Llama model yourself, on your own PC, with none of it going back to Meta. Expect to learn a few terms first. See Ollama vs LM Studio.

Company setups

Businesses that want one private AI for many staff run models on their own servers. That needs hardware and someone to maintain it, so it only makes sense at team scale. More in self-hosted AI assistants.

How to choose an offline AI

  1. Check what the app sends online, and why. A sign-in can be fine if it's only for the licence; GreenCube's sign-in only checks you paid and never sees your chats or files.
  2. Check it fits your PC. About 8 GB of RAM runs a small model; more RAM or a graphics card makes it much faster.
  3. Check what you need besides chat: reading PDFs or photos, making slides, working in your language.
  4. Check the price terms. A one-time price with a clear refund is easy to judge; so is free.

For speed, here's what we measured with GreenCube on the PCs we tested: 5–7 tokens a second on an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card (about reading speed), 14–18 on a 16 GB laptop, and 37–76 on most gaming PCs with a 6 GB or bigger card. To check yours, try Can my PC run AI?

What you give up

A model that fits on a laptop is smaller than the ones behind Meta AI or ChatGPT, and it shows on the hardest questions. It also doesn't know today's news unless you let it search; in GreenCube that's the optional "Online" switch, which is off unless you turn it on.

What you get back is an assistant that works on a plane, has no message limits, and keeps your chats on your own computer. Some people keep a cloud assistant for the hardest questions and use an offline one for everything personal. That's a perfectly sensible split.

Yes, if what you want is an assistant that doesn't send your chats to Meta. GreenCube does the same everyday jobs (chat, documents, study help) on your own Windows PC and keeps your chats there; it's smaller than Meta's cloud model, so weaker on the hardest questions.

Does GreenCube require an internet connection to work?

Only at the start. It needs the internet once, to sign in with Google or Microsoft (only to check you paid) and to download the AI. After that it works offline, and goes online only for updates or if you switch Online on.

Can GreenCube read images and PDFs?

Yes. It reads PDFs, Word files and photos on your PC without uploading them, and it picks the model for your computer, so there's nothing to set up.
