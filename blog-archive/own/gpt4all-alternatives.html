What's going on with GPT4All

GPT4All, by Nomic, was one of the first easy desktop apps for running AI on your own computer: free, MIT-licensed, with LocalDocs for chatting with your files (GitHub). Its releases page lists v3.10.0 from 24 February 2025 as the latest desktop release (releases). Nomic hasn't announced an end to the project on the pages we read, so we won't call it dead, only unupdated.

Why that matters: new AI model families usually need a newer engine to load, and every month of updates brings speed and bug fixes. An app that stops updating keeps working, but it falls further behind with each new model.

Pick by what you used GPT4All for

A free, open-source desktop chat

Jan is the closest swap: a desktop chat app that runs models itself, with a local OpenAI-compatible server, under Apache 2.0, for Windows, Mac with Apple chip and Linux (GitHub). Ollama (MIT) is a command-line engine with a desktop chat window on Windows and Mac. LM Studio is free too, but closed source.

LocalDocs: chatting with your own files

AnythingLLM (MIT) is built around document chat: PDF, TXT and DOCX, on Windows, Mac and Linux, with a Docker version for teams (GitHub). Jan, LM Studio and Msty also read your files.

Windows, without choosing models

GreenCube reads your graphics card and RAM and downloads the AI that fits, then answers questions about your PDFs, makes study guides and flashcards, reads photos and makes pictures, offline. €9.99 once, Windows 10 and 11. In our PDF test it answered 11 of 12 questions about long documents right, in about 15 seconds each, on a 16 GB laptop.

A Mac with an Intel chip

GPT4All supported macOS 12.6+. Jan and LM Studio need an Apple chip; Ollama's docs list Intel Macs as supported, CPU only, on macOS 14 or newer (Ollama docs).

How fast will it run?

Speed comes from your PC, not the app: the model runs on your graphics card or processor. We measured GreenCube on {{pcs}} real PCs: 5–7 tokens a second on an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card (about reading speed), 14–18 on a 16 GB laptop, and 37–76 on most gaming cards with 6 GB or more.
