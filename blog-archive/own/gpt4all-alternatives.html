I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Everything about the other apps below comes from their own websites, docs and GitHub pages, and we haven't benchmarked them against each other.

What's going on with GPT4All

GPT4All, made by Nomic, was one of the first easy desktop apps for running AI on your own computer: free, MIT-licensed, with LocalDocs for chatting with your files (GitHub). Its releases page lists v3.10.0 from 25 February 2025 as the latest desktop release (releases). Nomic hasn't announced an end to the project on the pages we read, so we won't call it dead, only unupdated.

That still matters. New families of AI models usually need a newer engine before they'll load, and each month of updates brings speed and bug fixes. An app that stops updating keeps working, but it falls further behind with every new model.

Pick by what you used GPT4All for

A free, open-source desktop chat

Jan is the closest swap. It's a desktop chat app that runs models itself and offers a local OpenAI-compatible server, under Apache 2.0, for Windows, Macs with Apple chips and Linux (GitHub). Ollama (MIT) is a command-line engine with a desktop chat window on Windows and Mac. LM Studio is free too, also for work, but it's closed source.

LocalDocs: chatting with your own files

AnythingLLM (MIT) is built around document chat. It reads PDF, TXT and DOCX, runs on Windows, Mac and Linux, and has a Docker version for teams (GitHub). Jan, LM Studio and Msty also read your files.

Windows, without choosing models

GreenCube reads your graphics card and RAM and downloads the AI that fits. Then it answers questions about your PDFs, makes study guides and flashcards, reads photos and makes pictures, offline. It costs €9.99 once and runs on Windows 10 and 11. In our PDF test it answered 11 of 12 questions about long documents right, in about 15 seconds each, on a 16 GB laptop.

A Mac with an Intel chip

GPT4All supported macOS 12.6 and newer. Jan and LM Studio need an Apple chip. Ollama's docs list Intel Macs as supported, on the processor only, with macOS 14 or newer (Ollama docs).

Three more worth knowing

Msty has a free plan and keeps your documents in what it calls Knowledge Stacks; the paid Aurum plan is $149 a year or $349 once. KoboldCpp is a single free .exe under AGPL-3.0 with text search, though its pages don't mention PDFs. Atomic Chat is free under Apache 2.0, with phone apps as well, but its privacy policy both denies telemetry and mentions PostHog statistics, which is worth knowing before you trust it with private files.

How fast will it run?

Speed comes from your PC more than the app, because the model runs on your graphics card or processor. We measured GreenCube on {{pcs}} real PCs: 5 to 7 tokens a second on an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card (about reading speed), 14 to 18 on a 16 GB laptop, and 37 to 76 on most gaming cards with 6 GB or more. To look up your exact card, try Can my PC run AI?
