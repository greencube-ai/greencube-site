There isn't one best AI for students, there's a best one for each job. For quick questions, use ChatGPT Free, which has had unlimited text since August 2026. For studying from your own notes with citations, NotebookLM is free and shows where each answer comes from. At university, claim Google's free year of AI Plus. For lots of PDFs, flashcards, pictures, offline study or notes you'd rather not upload, an AI on your own PC does the job.

That last kind is what I build, GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. The limits below come from each company's own pages, and the times are our own tests.

Free deals worth knowing

If you're at university and 18 or over, Google gives a year of AI Plus free, redeemable until 31 December 2026. It asks for a payment method and charges €4.99 a month after the year unless you cancel, so note the date (Google).

It's also worth asking your school. Many universities give students an AI through their own accounts, and work and school accounts are usually left out of training by default on the big services. That makes them more private than a personal free account.

Who sees your notes

On a free personal account, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Mistral, DeepSeek and Meta AI train on what you type by default. Gemini does while Keep Activity is on, NotebookLM doesn't unless you send feedback, and Claude only does if you allow it. All 9 are in which AI doesn't train on your chats.

If your notes belong to someone else, like your school or a classmate, check whether you may upload them at all.

Is it cheating?

Using AI to understand a topic, quiz yourself or get feedback on a draft is usually allowed. Handing in AI-written work as your own usually isn't. Rules differ by course, so check your academic integrity policy, and when in doubt, ask.

Will it run on your laptop?

8 GB of RAM works for chat and study, slowly. 16 GB is comfortable, and a gaming PC is fast. You can check yours in about 30 seconds with Can my PC run AI?
