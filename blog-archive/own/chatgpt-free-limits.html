What changed in August 2026

In the week of 10 August 2026, OpenAI made text chats unlimited on its Free and Go plans (Engadget). Its help page now says: "Free users have unlimited everyday text chats, subject to abuse-prevention safeguards. File uploads, image generation, voice, data analysis, and other tools have separate usage limits." (OpenAI Help Center).

So if you used to see "You've reached your limit" in 2025 just from chatting, that's mostly over. If it shows up in 2026, it's almost always because you sent a file, asked for a picture or used voice.

Why the caps are where they are

Reading a 40-page PDF or drawing a picture takes a cloud AI far more computing than a text reply, and on a free plan somebody has to pay for those servers. OpenAI says as much: keeping the Free and Go plans running "requires significant infrastructure", which is the reason it gives for the ads (OpenAI).

An AI on your own PC doesn't have that problem. Your graphics card or processor does the work, so there's nothing to ration and nobody counting. The catch is that it's only as fast as your PC (we measured {{pcs}} PCs here).

So what should you use?

If you mostly chat and rarely upload files, stay on ChatGPT Free. Unlimited text covers most questions, and its models are stronger than anything that fits on a PC.

Plus is worth it if you need the strongest models, voice or deep research every day, and $20 a month doesn't bother you.

An AI on your own PC suits people who study from PDFs and slides a lot, want pictures without a counter, often have bad Wi-Fi, or don't want another subscription. GreenCube is one: €9.99 once, no caps on chats, files or pictures, and it works offline after the first download. There are free apps that run AI on your PC too, which I've listed in which AIs have no limits at all.

Plenty of people mix the two: ChatGPT for quick questions, and an AI on their own PC for the heavy, repetitive work, like summarising a whole semester of PDFs.

Whatever you pick, the caps bite hardest right before exams, when you want every PDF turned into a summary and flashcards in one evening. Doing the heavy work early in the week, and keeping one tool without a counter for the night before, saves a lot of stress.
