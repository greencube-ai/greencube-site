The best local AI model for your PC is the biggest one that fits whole on your graphics card with about 1.5 GB to spare. With no graphics card, pick a small model that runs in your RAM. A smaller model fully on the card beats a bigger one that spills over: when part of a model didn't fit, we measured it running 6 times slower.

Below is the pick for each kind of PC, with speeds we measured on {{pcs}} real PCs. One thing to know first: we've only timed the Gemma 4 models that GreenCube ships (I build GreenCube), so those are the only models I give speeds for. The sizing rule works for any model family.

Why "fits" matters more than "biggest"

When a model spills out of the card, it can end up slower than having no card at all. On an 8 GB RX 5700 XT, Gemma 4 12B wrote 35.6 tokens a second fully on the card, 5.9 with part of it off the card, and 4.3 with even less on it. That last number is slower than a smaller model running with no card. The whole test is in how much VRAM you need for AI.

Fitting for one question isn't the same as fitting for a chat, either. The same 12B model fit on the 8 GB card for a short question, then crashed every time the conversation grew, because the conversation needs room on the card too. That's what the 1.5 GB of headroom is for.

And small is often good enough. On a 16 GB laptop, Gemma 4 E2B gave a complete study answer in 20 seconds and got 5 of 5 quick fact checks right. E4B explained things better but took 112 seconds. There's more in E2B vs E4B, measured.

How to check your VRAM

  1. Press Ctrl + Shift + Esc to open Task Manager.
  2. Go to Performance, then GPU.
  3. Look at Dedicated GPU memory: that's your VRAM. If there's no dedicated memory, use a small model in RAM.

Free apps like LM Studio, Jan and Ollama let you choose the model yourself (12 apps compared). If you'd rather not pick at all, GreenCube reads your card and RAM and chooses a model that fits with room to spare.
