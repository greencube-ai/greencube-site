Secure AI on a PC means an AI that runs on your own computer, so your prompts and files are processed and saved there instead of on a company's server. It's practical today on a Windows PC with 8 GB of RAM or more. To make it genuinely secure, pick an app that is clear about when it goes online, run a model that fits your hardware, and protect the PC itself with disk encryption and a screen lock.

I build GreenCube, one of these apps, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. This guide covers what local AI protects you from and what it doesn't, the hardware you need, how to judge an app, and a short hardening checklist.

What "secure" means here

With a cloud AI, everything you type goes to the provider's servers, where it's kept under their policy and, depending on your settings, may be used to train future models (is ChatGPT private?). With a local AI the model runs on your PC. There's no copy on a server to leak, to be read by staff, or to fall under new terms if the company changes its policy.

That protects you from the provider and from breaches on their side. It doesn't protect you from risks on your own machine: someone using your unlocked laptop, malware that steals files, or a sync folder that quietly copies your chats to the cloud. Local AI moves the security question onto your PC, so the PC is what you harden.

Who it's for

People with documents they'd rather not upload: contracts and client files, medical or financial papers, students' work, unpublished writing. Students who want to study from PDFs without depending on Wi-Fi. Anyone who simply doesn't want a record of their chats on someone else's server.

It's a poor fit if you need the strongest models available (the big cloud AIs are clearly better on the hardest problems), live information from the web, or one shared workspace for a whole team.

What hardware you need

The main limit is memory. A model has to fit in RAM, or better in your graphics card's own memory (VRAM), where it runs much faster. As a rule of thumb, a 4-bit (quantized) model needs a little over half a gigabyte per billion parameters, plus room for the conversation: about 4 to 5 GB for a 7B model and around 9 GB for a 14B model.

If a model is bigger than your VRAM, most apps split it between the graphics card and normal RAM. It still works but gets much slower, so a smaller model that fits completely is usually the better choice. A fast SSD also helps models load quickly.

How to judge an app

  • It says plainly that the AI runs on your device, and lists when it goes online: first download, updates, sign-in, optional web search.
  • It keeps working with the Wi-Fi off. Test this yourself.
  • It tells you where chats and files are saved, and lets you delete them.
  • It doesn't send usage data or crash reports you didn't agree to.
  • Its models come from a known source. If you download models yourself, use the official pages and compare the published checksum.

A hardening checklist

  1. Turn on disk encryption: BitLocker on Windows Pro, or Device encryption on Windows Home if your PC supports it. It protects your chats if the laptop is lost or stolen.
  2. Use a screen lock and a strong sign-in. Physical access to an unlocked PC gets past every other control.
  3. Keep Windows and your antivirus up to date. Malware that steals files doesn't care whether the AI is local.
  4. Keep sensitive documents in their own folder, and make sure that folder isn't syncing to a cloud drive such as OneDrive unless you mean it to.
  5. If you want proof that an app stays offline, watch its connections in Windows Resource Monitor, or block it in Windows Defender Firewall. Blocking it also blocks its updates, so allow it again now and then.
  6. Install updates for the AI app itself. Its engine is software like any other and gets security fixes.

What you give up

A model that fits on a PC is smaller than the ones behind ChatGPT or Gemini, and it shows on long maths, tricky code and very niche facts. It doesn't know today's news unless the app can search the web. And a slow PC is slow: on an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card, GreenCube writes about 5 to 7 tokens a second on the PCs we tested, roughly reading speed. For reading a contract, summarising a paper or drafting a private letter, that trade is usually fine. For the hardest problems, use a cloud AI and keep anything sensitive out of it.
