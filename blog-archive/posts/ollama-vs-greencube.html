I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Ollama is a great piece of software, and for some people it's clearly the better choice. Here's how to tell which one you are.

What each one is

Ollama is a free, open-source tool for running AI models on your own computer. For years it was mostly used from the command line, and other apps connect to it through its local API. Since mid-2025 it also has a simple chat window on Windows and Mac, where you can download models and drop in files. Which model you run, and how big, is up to you.

GreenCube is a finished Windows app aimed at people who don't want to make those choices. It checks your PC and installs the model that fits: Cube 2B on smaller PCs, Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma. Then it works offline.

Where Ollama is better

It costs nothing, and it runs on Mac and Linux as well as Windows. It also gives you choice: if you have a powerful PC, you can run bigger models than the ones GreenCube picks, and try new ones the week they come out. And because other software can talk to it, it's the engine behind many other local AI apps; developers use it to build their own tools. If you like comparing models and reading about settings, you'll probably enjoy Ollama more.

Where GreenCube is the better fit

GreenCube is for people who want the result without the research. You don't need to know what a model size or a quantisation is, because it decides for your PC. It's also about more than chat: it turns a request into a slide deck that saves as PowerPoint in one of 15 styles, makes flashcards and quizzes from your PDFs, draws labelled diagrams and makes pictures, all on your computer. The app is in 19 languages.

The trade-off is that it's Windows only, costs €9.99, and you sign in once with Google or Microsoft so it can check that you paid. That account never sees your chats or files.

Privacy: about the same

On privacy the two are close. Both write answers on your own machine and keep your chats there, so neither sends your conversations to a server while you work offline. GreenCube only goes online for updates, or if you switch on Online for web lookups. With either tool, your data is then as safe as your PC, so a password, a locked screen and disk encryption matter. A five-minute test

Don't take my word for it. Whichever you try, run the same quick test:

  1. Ask three or four questions you'd really ask during a normal week.
  2. Give it a real document, like a two-page PDF, and ask for a summary.
  3. Switch the Wi-Fi off and do it again. It should behave the same.
  4. Ask yourself: was it fast enough, was the answer right, and could you find everything without a manual?

If GreenCube doesn't pass for you, there's a 15-day refund: email support@greencube.app.

Who should pick which

Pick Ollama if you're comfortable with a bit of setup, want to choose your own models, use a Mac or Linux, need an API for other apps, or simply don't want to pay. Ollama vs LM Studio compares it with the other popular free option.

Pick GreenCube if you're on Windows, want something that works the moment it's installed, and want decks, flashcards, quizzes and pictures as well as chat. Both are far smaller than ChatGPT or Claude, so for the very hardest problems, a cloud AI is still stronger.
