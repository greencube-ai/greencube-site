Claude is the better AI for hard work: careful writing, coding and long documents, with web search and apps on every device. GreenCube is the better fit when privacy and cost come first: it runs on your own Windows PC, works offline, keeps your chats there, and costs €9.99 (US$9.99) once instead of $20 a month for Claude Pro.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. This post compares the two by what they can do, where your data goes and what hardware each needs. If your main question is money, I go through the cost over a year in GreenCube vs Claude: what you save and what you give up.

How each one works

Claude runs on Anthropic's servers. Every message and file you send travels to a data centre, a very large model writes the answer, and the chat is saved in your account. That's how it can offer models far bigger than any laptop could hold, and why it needs a connection every time.

GreenCube downloads a smaller AI onto your PC on first launch, then writes every answer on your own processor and graphics card. It picks the model for your machine: Cube 2B on smaller PCs, Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma.

What Claude does better

Quite a lot, honestly. Claude is one of the strongest models you can use for writing that needs judgement, code that spans many files, and analysis of long reports or transcripts. It can read a whole contract or a stack of papers in one go and keep track of the details, which a small model on a laptop can't match.

It also comes with the things a cloud service is good at: web search for current information, Projects to keep files and instructions together, apps on your phone and Mac, and plans for teams. If your day is mostly hard thinking with an AI beside you, Claude is the better tool.

Privacy: what leaves your computer

With Claude, everything you type or upload goes to Anthropic and is stored with your account. You can delete chats, and Anthropic's consumer settings let you choose whether your chats are used to improve its models. Business and enterprise plans come with their own terms. That's a reasonable setup for most work, but the data still leaves your device, and some people and employers aren't allowed or willing to accept that for certain files.

With GreenCube, the internet is used once to sign in (Google or Microsoft, only to check you paid; it never sees your chats or files) and once to download the AI. After that it only goes online for updates, or if you switch on Online for web lookups. Your chats, files and images are saved on your PC, and there's no server on our side that holds them.

Keeping data local moves the responsibility to you. A laptop with no password protects nothing, so lock your screen and turn on disk encryption. Our offline AI security guide covers the basics.

Hardware: what each one needs

Claude runs on any device with a browser or its app, because the heavy lifting happens on Anthropic's machines. GreenCube needs a Windows 10 or 11 PC with about 8 GB of memory or more, and the speed depends on what you have. These are the speeds we measured on the PCs we tested:

Who should pick which

Pick Claude if you code most days, write or edit long and demanding pieces, work through big documents, need current information from the web, or switch between a phone, a Mac and a PC.

Pick GreenCube if you're a student or professional working on Windows with material you'd rather keep on your own machine, if you often work offline, or if you want no limits and no monthly bill. It's good at study help, drafts, everyday questions, and making decks, quizzes, flashcards and pictures.

The two also work well together: GreenCube for private files and offline days, Claude for the problems that need the biggest model.
