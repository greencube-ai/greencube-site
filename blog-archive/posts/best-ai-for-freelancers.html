I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. This post is about what it's good for in freelance work, what your laptop needs, and where it falls short.

Why freelancers need a different answer

Most lists of AI tools for freelancers point to ChatGPT, Claude or a writing tool like Jasper. They're cloud subscriptions, and they're fine for plenty of work. But if you handle client contracts, unreleased creative work or financial documents, every prompt goes to another company's servers, and your client may not have agreed to that.

An AI on your own laptop changes the question. The client's files stay on your machine, it works on a train with no signal, and you pay once instead of every month. ChatGPT Plus, for comparison, is $20 a month. What you give up is some raw capability.

What GreenCube does

GreenCube runs the AI on your PC and saves your chats and files there. It answers questions and drafts text, reads PDFs, Word files and photos (a screenshot of a messy spreadsheet, a scanned contract), and makes documents, slide decks that save as PowerPoint, diagrams and images, all on your computer. It's for Windows 10 and 11; there's no Mac version.

You need internet once: to sign in with a Google or Microsoft account, which only checks that you paid and never sees your chats or files, and to download the AI on first launch. After that it works offline. It goes online again only for updates, or if you switch "Online" on for web lookups.

There's no model to choose any more. GreenCube checks your PC and picks one: Cube 2B on smaller laptops, Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma.

What freelancers use it for

The jobs that make an offline assistant worth having are the repetitive ones: summarising a client's 20-page brief before a call, pulling the payment and termination clauses out of a contract, turning notes into a proposal outline, drafting a recap email, or building a quick slide deck for a pitch.

A routine that covers a lot of it: open the client's PDF, ask for a one-page summary, ask for a proposal outline based on that summary, then refine the draft yourself. Keep the prompts that work. A small set you reuse, one for contract review, one for client recaps, saves more time than any clever one-off.

A good way to start is one workflow for two weeks, contract review or client recaps, before you try to use it for everything.

Where it falls short

A model small enough for a laptop doesn't compete with the big cloud AIs on long, multi-step reasoning or research. For first drafts, summaries and sorting through documents that gap rarely matters; for a long argument that has to be airtight, it might. It doesn't know today's news unless Online is on. And on an older 8 GB laptop it's slow. If those limits bite, use a cloud AI for the material you're happy to share.

How to judge any offline AI tool

  • Does it really work with the Wi-Fi off? Turn it off and try a chat and a document.
  • Does it say plainly what sign-in is for, and what (if anything) leaves your computer?
  • Does it state which file types it reads?
  • Is "one-time" really one-time, with no renewal hidden in the terms?
  • Is there a refund if it doesn't fit your work? GreenCube's is 15 days, by emailing support@greencube.app.
