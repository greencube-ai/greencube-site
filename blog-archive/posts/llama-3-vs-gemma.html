Neither family wins everywhere, and your graphics card decides more than the benchmarks do. Gemma is the better start on modest hardware, especially if you need the model to read images: Gemma 3 from 4B up and Gemma 4 take images natively, and Gemma 4 comes under the Apache 2.0 licence. Llama 3 is the better start if you want the largest ecosystem of fine-tunes and tools, or a 70B model and the 40 GB or more of graphics memory to run it.

Here are the checkpoints worth knowing, what each needs, and how to test them before you commit.

The checkpoints to shortlist

Llama 3 family (Meta)

  • Llama 3 (April 2024): 8B and 70B, text only, with an 8K-token context.
  • Llama 3.1: 8B, 70B and 405B, with the context raised to 128K tokens.
  • Llama 3.2: small text models at 1B and 3B for laptops and phones, plus 11B and 90B vision models that read images.
  • Llama 3.3: a 70B instruct model, the strongest of the Llama 3 line that one big card can hold.

Meta has since released Llama 4, but Llama 3 is still the version most local tools, guides and fine-tunes are built around.

Gemma family (Google)

  • Gemma 2: 2B, 9B and 27B, text only. Still fine for older machines and experiments.
  • Gemma 3 (March 2025): 1B, 4B, 12B and 27B. From 4B up it reads images, with a 128K-token context.
  • Gemma 4: the newest family, from small E2B and E4B models built for laptops and phones up to larger ones for big graphics cards, released under Apache 2.0.

How they differ

Images are the clearest difference at small sizes. Gemma 3 4B and the small Gemma 4 models read images in the same model, while the small Llama 3.2 models (1B and 3B) are text only, and Llama's vision models start at 11B.

Ecosystem is Llama's advantage. It has been public longer, so there are more fine-tuned versions, training recipes and guides for it, and every runtime (llama.cpp, Ollama, LM Studio, vLLM) supports it well. Gemma is well supported too, but you'll find fewer community variants.

At the very top, Llama goes bigger. Llama 3.1 405B and the 70B models are a different class from a 27B Gemma 3, especially on code and long reasoning, but they need server-class hardware. For coding, bigger usually means better in both families; if code is your main job, also look at models trained specifically for it.

Published benchmark scores change with every release and every quantization, so treat them as pointers. The only test that counts is your own prompts on your own card.

Licences

Llama models use Meta's Llama Community License. It allows commercial use, but it adds conditions: an acceptable use policy, attribution rules, and a clause that very large companies (over 700 million monthly users) need a separate licence from Meta. Gemma 2 and 3 use Google's Gemma Terms of Use, which also allow commercial use with a list of prohibited uses. Gemma 4 moved to Apache 2.0, a standard open-source licence with no extra use policy. If you're shipping a product, read the actual licence text.

How to choose and test

  1. Start from your hardware. If it doesn't run well on what you have, nothing else matters.
  2. Decide whether you need image input. If yes, that narrows the list quickly.
  3. Check the licence against what you'll do with it.
  4. Run 20 or so real prompts from your own work through two or three candidates, at the quantization you'll actually use, and read the answers side by side.
  5. Measure speed on your card. Tokens per second on someone else's machine tells you little.

What we use

I build GreenCube, a Windows app that runs AI offline, so here's what we went with, for what it's worth. GreenCube runs Gemma 4: Cube 2B is built on Gemma 4 E2B for smaller PCs, and Cube 4B on Gemma 4 E4B for PCs with a graphics card, and the app picks one for you. On the PCs we tested, it wrote 5–7 tokens a second on an 8 GB laptop with no card, 14–18 on a 16 GB laptop, and 37–76 on most gaming PCs with a 6 GB or bigger card. If you'd rather run Llama or Gemma checkpoints yourself and pick them by hand, Ollama or LM Studio is the way to go.

Gemma and Gemini are not the same thing

People mix these up. Gemini is Google's cloud assistant and family of hosted models; you use it through Google's apps and API. Gemma is a family of open models Google publishes for anyone to download and run, built from the same research. You can run Gemma on your own PC, offline; you can't do that with Gemini.
