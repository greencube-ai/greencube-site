A detailed look at how these tools compare

No single tool wins across every dimension. The best writing tools separate themselves on language depth, workflow fit, and what they prioritize: correction, generation, or readability.

Grammar depth and accuracy

ProWritingAid leads for writers who want craft-level feedback. Its 25+ writing reports analyze pacing, sentence variety, overused words, and dialogue tags, not just comma placement. That depth is genuinely useful for novelists and long-form journalists. It also does not use your text to train AI models, which matters if you're working on unpublished manuscripts.

LanguageTool punches hard on pure grammar accuracy across languages. Its free tier checks spelling, basic punctuation, and some style issues; the Premium tier unlocks the full rule set. The browser add-on works across Chrome, Firefox, and Safari, and the desktop app covers Microsoft Word and most Mac applications.

Hemingway Editor takes a different angle entirely. It doesn't suggest corrections so much as it highlights problems: sentences colored by complexity level, passive voice flagged in red, adverbs in blue. You fix the prose yourself. Writers who want to develop their own editing instincts often prefer this over tools that auto-correct.

Paraphrasing and AI rewriting

QuillBot is the clear leader here. Its free tier includes paraphrasing, translation, and AI-detection checking, with no word-count cap on basic grammar. Wordtune offers multiple AI-generated phrasing alternatives per sentence, which is useful when you know something reads awkwardly but can't pinpoint why. Neither replaces a grammar checker for technical accuracy, but both save time on rewrites.

Writesonic and Jasper are primarily content generation platforms with grammar features bolted on. They work well for marketing teams producing high volumes of copy, but they're not the right choice if your main need is editing existing prose.

Plagiarism and originality checking

CopyLeaks and QueText are the specialists here. CopyLeaks uses AI-powered detection and works across multiple file formats, making it practical for publishers and content managers. QueText's color-coded results are easy to read at a glance, though its grammar correction is limited. PaperRater combines both plagiarism checking and grammar feedback in a free, no-signup interface aimed at students.

Multilingual support

For writers working across languages, the gap between tools is significant. LanguageTool supports over 30 languages and dialects, including English in six regional variants (US, UK, Canadian, Australian, New Zealand, and South African), Spanish, German, French, Dutch, Portuguese, and more. DeepL Write pairs grammar correction with the DeepL translation engine, making it the strongest option for business writers who draft in multiple languages. Reverso adds a contextual dictionary and conjugation tools on top of grammar checking, which is particularly useful for language learners and translators.

Pro Tip: If you write in a language other than English, LanguageTool and DeepL Write are the two tools worth testing first. Most others are English-only or treat other languages as secondary.

How to choose the right tool for your writing needs

The choice comes down to four questions, and answering them honestly narrows the field fast.

What are you writing?

Long-form authors (novelists, journalists, technical writers) get the most from ProWritingAid. Its analysis across chapters and large files is something most grammar tools can't replicate, since many tools optimized for short-form content lose accuracy and context on long documents. Business writers producing emails, reports, and presentations tend to prefer Hemingway Editor or Wordtune for quick clarity fixes. Marketing teams generating volume copy lean toward Jasper or Writesonic.

What languages do you write in?

English-only writers have the widest selection. Writers working in Spanish, German, French, or other languages should start with LanguageTool or DeepL Write and evaluate from there. Tools like grammar checkers for multilingual documents are worth testing if your work spans multiple languages regularly.

What's your budget?

  • Free with no signup: Slick Write, SentenceCheckup, Hemingway Editor (web), Virtual Writing Tutor
  • Free tier with limits: QuillBot, LanguageTool, PaperRater, Wordtune, EditGPT
  • Paid only: WhiteSmoke, PerfectIt, Writer.com (enterprise)
  • One-time purchase: Hemingway Editor (desktop app)

Do you need grammar correction, AI rewriting, or both?

AI rewriting tools and grammar checkers serve different functions. Rewriting tools like Wordtune and QuillBot's paraphraser change phrasing and tone but often miss technical grammar errors. Dedicated grammar tools like LanguageTool and PerfectIt catch mechanical errors but won't rewrite your prose. Most writers end up using one of each, which is worth factoring into your budget.

Questions to ask before committing to a tool:

  • Does it integrate with the apps I already use (Word, Google Docs, Chrome)?
  • Does it support the language or dialect I write in?
  • Does the free tier cover my typical document length?
  • Does it send my text to external servers, and does that matter for my content?
  • Am I primarily editing existing text or generating new content?

What experts and analysts say about picking a writing tool

The clearest expert consensus: match the tool to your primary writing medium, not to a feature checklist. Long-form authors benefit most from ProWritingAid's craft-level reports. Business writers get more practical value from Hemingway or Wordtune's clarity and tone controls. ESL writers and multilingual professionals should prioritize LanguageTool or DeepL Write over any English-centric tool.

That distinction matters in practice. ChatGPT can fix grammar, rewrite paragraphs, and suggest structure, but it can also introduce errors or hallucinate corrections on longer documents. Using it alongside a dedicated grammar checker like LanguageTool or ProWritingAid catches what each misses.

Privacy is a growing concern that analysts are starting to take seriously. Cloud-based grammar tools process your text on remote servers, which creates real exposure risk for unpublished manuscripts, legal documents, or proprietary business content. ProWritingAid's policy of not training AI on user text is a meaningful differentiator in that context. Offline tools go further by keeping text off the network entirely.

Integration ecosystems drive adoption more than raw feature counts. A tool with slightly weaker grammar detection that works inside your existing Word or Google Docs workflow will get used. A more powerful tool that requires copying text into a separate interface often won't.

Support quality and update frequency across tools

Support quality varies sharply between free and paid tiers, and between consumer and enterprise products.

LanguageTool releases updates frequently and maintains active community forums. Premium and Team subscribers get email support; free users rely on documentation and community threads.

ProWritingAid has a responsive support team and publishes regular product updates. Its blog and help center are genuinely useful, covering both tool features and writing craft.

QuillBot offers email support for paid users and a help center for free users. Update frequency is high, with new AI features added regularly since its paraphrasing tool became its flagship feature.

Writer.com and Sapling provide dedicated account management for enterprise clients. That level of support doesn't exist for individual users, which is by design: both products are built for team deployment.

Hemingway Editor is a small operation. Updates are infrequent, support is minimal, and the tool hasn't changed dramatically in years. That's not necessarily a problem since the core functionality is stable and the tool does what it does well.

Jasper and Writesonic update aggressively, adding new AI models and templates regularly. Support for paid plans is responsive; free tier users get limited help.

PaperRater, Slick Write, and SentenceCheckup are lightweight tools with minimal support infrastructure. They work for what they are, but don't expect active development or fast responses to issues.

PerfectIt stands out for professional-grade support and regular updates tied to major style guide revisions (Chicago, APA, AMA). Editors who rely on it for client work report that the development team is responsive to feature requests.

ChatGPT and EditGPT update on OpenAI's release cycle. ChatGPT Plus subscribers get priority access to new models; free users may experience slower performance during peak times.

FAQ

Is there a better option than Grammarly?

For specific use cases, yes. QuillBot's free tier beats Grammarly's free version on usability and lacks the aggressive upselling. LanguageTool outperforms Grammarly on multilingual support, covering over 30 languages and dialects compared to Grammarly's English focus.

Is ChatGPT or Grammarly better for grammar checking?

Grammarly is more reliable for catching mechanical grammar errors in real time within documents. ChatGPT is more flexible and can handle rewriting, tone, and structure, but requires careful review since it can introduce errors, especially in longer documents.

Will I get flagged for AI if I use a grammar checker?

Standard grammar checkers like LanguageTool, ProWritingAid, and Hemingway Editor correct errors without generating new content, so they don't typically trigger AI-detection tools. AI rewriting tools like QuillBot's paraphraser or Wordtune may produce output that AI detectors flag, depending on how heavily you use them.

What is the most private grammar tool available?

Offline tools that don't send text to external servers offer the strongest privacy. ProWritingAid does not train AI on user text. Greencube runs entirely on your own computer with no cloud connection at all, which is the most private option for writers handling sensitive or proprietary content.

What's the best free Grammarly substitute for students?

PaperRater combines grammar checking and plagiarism detection with no account required, making it practical for academic writing. QuillBot's free tier is also strong for grammar and paraphrasing without a subscription.
