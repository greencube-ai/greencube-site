How to pick the right alternative for your workflow

Start by asking what you're actually trading away for convenience. A UCL analysis of browser-based AI assistants found that these tools are excellent for quick inline edits and multitasking across tabs, but that convenience often comes at the cost of deep data ownership, since browser extensions typically route your text through a cloud server.

  1. Check the privacy posture. Decide whether your work involves sensitive documents that shouldn't leave your machine.
  2. Match the interface to your habit. Inline overlays suit fast edits; a workspace or desktop app suits longer sessions.
  3. Weigh model flexibility. BYOK tools give you model choice and cost transparency; fixed-plan tools trade that for simplicity.
  4. Test document and media support. Try uploading a real PDF or a YouTube link before committing.
  5. Watch for usage caps and upgrade friction. Read the fine print on what counts as a "credit."
  6. Consider your device's memory footprint. Some browser extensions are known for memory leaks during long sessions.

Before you commit to any tool, run a short trial. Open a separate browser profile, feed the assistant a real PDF you'd normally work with, time how long a typical response takes and push it until you hit a cap if one exists. This takes about seven to ten minutes and tells you more than any marketing page will.

Watch for red flags during that trial: hidden caps that only appear after a few days of use, billing that isn't clearly explained upfront, slow or absent customer support, and browser tabs that keep eating memory the longer they stay open. Reviews flag exactly these issues often enough that they're worth checking deliberately rather than discovering by accident.

Pro Tip: Test any new AI tool in a separate browser profile or a temporary workspace so it never touches your main bookmarks, saved logins, or existing chat history.
