For long documents and harder thinking, a full chat app such as Claude, ChatGPT or Perplexity is better than any sidebar. I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Here are the real alternatives, grouped by what they do, with the catch for each.

Browser assistants that work on the page

Monica is mainly a browser extension: you select text on a page and ask it to summarise, rewrite or translate. These tools work the same way.

Merlin puts a command bar over any page, so you can call it up without switching tabs. It suits people who jump between many tabs a day. Its free tier runs out quickly if you use it heavily.

MaxAI is built around selecting text and acting on it right there, which saves the copy and paste. Handy for summarising articles and translating emails. It depends on the page loading cleanly, so complex sites can trip it up.

Sider, Cabina.AI and NOIZ sit in the same group of in-page helpers. Try one or two side by side on the sites you use most and keep whichever feels least in the way.

The catch for all of them: an extension reads the page you're on and sends what you ask about to its servers. Researchers at UCL looked at AI browser assistants and raised exactly this privacy concern. For public articles that may not matter. For your bank or medical pages, it does.

Bring your own API key

HARPA AI and Ask Bar let you plug in your own key from OpenAI, Anthropic or another provider. You pay the provider directly for what you use, and you choose the model. That's appealing if you want to see exactly what you're billed for. The downside is the setup: getting a key, adding a payment method, keeping an eye on usage. Your text still goes to whichever provider you connect. If you like this model, TypingMind and its alternatives work the same way in a full chat window.

Full chat assistants for deeper work

Claude is strong with long documents and multi-step requests. ChatGPT is the all-rounder most people already know. Perplexity is built for searching the web and answering with sources attached. Poe gives you several of these models in one place. All four are better at real reasoning than a sidebar, but they aren't made for quick edits inside other pages, and heavy use means a paid plan. Writing and translation tools

If you mostly used Monica to fix and polish text, a writing tool may be the better swap. Grammarly and Wordtune focus on grammar, tone and rephrasing. Lunabot bundles writing helpers and plagiarism checks into a browser toolbox aimed at students and content writers. DeepL is the one to try if translation is the main job. An AI that stays on your computer

Every tool above runs in the cloud. GreenCube works differently: it's a Windows app that runs the AI on your own PC, so your chats and files are saved there and never sent to anyone. How to choose

  1. Decide whether anything you'll paste in is sensitive. If it is, rule out tools that send it to a server.
  2. Match the shape to your habit: a sidebar for quick edits, a chat app for long sessions.
  3. Read what counts as a "credit" or "query" on the free plan before you rely on it.
  4. Try it on your real work for a few days, since caps and quirks often show up after the first day.

My own view: if you handle client contracts, medical notes or unpublished writing, keep them on your own machine. If you mostly summarise public articles across ten tabs, a browser assistant like Merlin or MaxAI will feel quicker.
