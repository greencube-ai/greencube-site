If you like DeepSeek but don't want your chats stored on its servers, the best alternative is an AI that runs on your own Windows PC: free apps like LM Studio and Ollama (which can even run smaller versions of DeepSeek's own model), or GreenCube, which costs €9.99 once and picks the model for you. If you just want a different cloud chatbot, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Mistral's Le Chat do the same job, but your chats then go to their servers instead.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Here's why people switch, what the local options are, and what your PC needs.

Why people look for a DeepSeek alternative

DeepSeek's chatbot is free and capable, but it runs on DeepSeek's servers, and its privacy policy says it collects, processes and stores personal data in the People's Republic of China. For a lot of people, and for many employers, that alone rules it out for work files.

The good news is that DeepSeek also publishes its models openly. DeepSeek-R1 is released under the MIT licence, so anyone can download it and run it on their own hardware, where nothing goes back to DeepSeek.

Running DeepSeek itself on your PC

The full DeepSeek-R1 is far too big for a home computer: it has 671 billion parameters. Alongside it, DeepSeek released smaller "distilled" versions, from 1.5 billion to 70 billion parameters, built on Qwen and Llama models and trained to imitate R1's step-by-step reasoning. The small ones run in LM Studio or Ollama on an ordinary PC. They're much weaker than the DeepSeek you use online, and because they "think out loud" before answering, they're slower to reach the answer.

Local apps to run any model

The free ones

LM Studio, Ollama, Jan and GPT4All are free. You pick a model from a long list (DeepSeek's distilled models, Llama, Qwen, Gemma and many more), download it and chat, offline if you like. They suit people who enjoy comparing models and don't mind learning what a file like "Q4_K_M" means. See Ollama vs LM Studio and LM Studio alternatives.

GreenCube

GreenCube is for people who want it to just work. It checks your PC and picks the model that fits: Cube 2B on smaller PCs, Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma (it doesn't run DeepSeek models). Besides chat it makes slide decks, documents, quizzes, flashcards and images, and reads PDFs, Word files and photos. It needs the internet once, to sign in with Google or Microsoft (only to check you paid; it never sees your chats or files) and to download the AI. Then it works offline, with chats and files saved on your PC. It's for Windows 10 and 11 and costs €9.99 once, with a 15-day refund.

Your graphics card matters more than anything else

A local model runs fastest when the whole file fits in your graphics card's own memory (VRAM). If it doesn't fit, part of it spills into normal RAM and the speed drops sharply. So pick the model by your card, not by how impressive its name is. You can see your VRAM in Task Manager, under Performance, then GPU, as "Dedicated GPU memory". More in how much VRAM for AI.

No graphics card is fine too; a small model runs in RAM, just slower. Here's how fast GreenCube wrote on the PCs we tested:

What you give up

A model small enough for a PC is less capable than the big online DeepSeek, ChatGPT or Claude, and it shows on long maths, tricky code and very niche facts. It also doesn't know today's news unless it can search; in GreenCube that's the optional "Online" switch.

A local AI is also a standalone tool. It won't plug into your company's software the way a cloud API can. What it's good at is the personal work: reading a PDF before a meeting, drafting from notes you can't upload, studying on a train, with no limits on how much you use it.
