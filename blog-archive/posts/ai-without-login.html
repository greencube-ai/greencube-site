Yes. ChatGPT and some other chatbots let you type a question on their website and get an answer without creating an account. There are two catches. A guest chat usually lives only in that browser tab, so copy anything you want to keep before you close it. And "no account" doesn't mean private: what you type still goes to the company's servers.

Where you can use AI without an account

The quickest route is a chatbot's own website in guest mode. ChatGPT's site, for example, lets you chat while logged out. Some AI companies and research labs also run demo pages where you can try a model without signing in, and a few sites put several models on one page so you can compare their answers.

The other route is an app that runs the AI on your own computer. It takes an install and a one-time download, but after that your chats are saved on your PC instead of in a browser tab.

What "no login" actually means

Skipping the sign-in means the company isn't tying your chat to a named account. It doesn't mean your words stay on your device. Your question still travels to their servers, and the answer is written there.

Guest chats can also be used to improve the company's models, depending on its policy and on whether it lets you opt out. Look at the tool's settings or privacy page before you paste anything personal.

And guest history is fragile. It's usually kept in your browser, so closing the tab, clearing cookies or switching to another device can wipe it. Don't expect to find the conversation again next week.

How to get an answer now, and keep it

  1. Open the chatbot's website and skip the sign-in if it's optional.
  2. Ask a clear, specific question. If the session resets, the context you built goes with it.
  3. Copy the parts of the answer you want into a note or a document straight away, not three questions later.
  4. Ask follow-ups in the same tab. Starting a new chat or reloading the page usually loses the thread.

If your question involves a PDF, a scan or a photo, think twice. Even where a guest chat accepts files, the file goes to a server, with no account settings around it.

When guest chat is fine, and when it isn't

Guest chat is good for quick, low-stakes things: checking a fact, a bit of brainstorming, trying out a model. It's the wrong place for passwords, client records, medical or legal papers, or anything you'd mind a company keeping. It's also a poor fit for longer projects, because there's nothing to come back to.

My own rule is simple. Guest chat for anything I'd be fine losing, and an app on my own PC for anything I want to find again.

An AI on your own PC

A local AI turns the default around. The model runs on your hardware, so your questions and files don't travel anywhere, and your chat history stays on your disk.
