A no-setup AI is one you can start using without choosing models, changing settings or opening a terminal. There are two kinds: AI that runs inside a browser tab with nothing to install, and desktop apps you install once that then pick and download everything themselves. If you also want it private, offline and paid for only once, a desktop app is the safer bet.

I build GreenCube, a desktop app of the second kind, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Below: how the two kinds differ, what to check before you trust one, and what setting up GreenCube actually involves.

Two kinds of "no setup"

AI in a browser tab

Some tools run a small model inside your browser using WebGPU, which lets a web page use your graphics card. You open the page, wait while the model downloads into the browser, and start chatting. There's nothing to install and no admin rights needed, which helps on a school or work laptop where installs are blocked. WebLLM is a well-known open-source project that works this way.

The catches: the model sits in the browser's cache and can be wiped with it, speed depends on your browser and graphics card, and your text lives in a browser tab, where extensions with access to that page can read it. The page's code also comes from a website each time you load it, so you're trusting that site every visit.

A desktop app

You install one program, it downloads a model to your drive once, and it runs like any other app. It doesn't depend on a browser, and the model stays put until you delete it. The catch is that you need permission to install software on the computer.

How to tell whether it's really private

Words like "secure" and "private" on a product page don't tell you much. These do:

  • It says plainly that the AI runs on your device, and when (if ever) it goes online.
  • It still answers with the Wi-Fi off. Try it.
  • It says where your chats and files are saved.
  • The price is clear: free, a one-time payment, or a subscription, with no surprise credits.
  • It lists the minimum hardware, so you know before paying whether it will run.

Be wary if the privacy policy talks about processing your prompts on "our servers", or says nothing about where the model runs.

What setting up GreenCube looks like

  1. Pay on greencube.app (€9.99, once) with the same email you'll sign in with.
  2. Download the Windows installer and run it.
  3. Open GreenCube and sign in with Google or Microsoft. That's only to check you paid; it never sees your chats or files.
  4. On first launch it checks your PC and downloads the AI that fits, a few gigabytes, once: Cube 2B on smaller PCs, Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma.
  5. Start typing, or drop in a PDF, Word file or photo.

From then on it works offline. It only goes online for updates, or if you switch "Online" on for web lookups. Chats, files and images are saved on your PC. Besides chat, it makes slide decks (saved as PowerPoint), documents, quizzes, flashcards, labelled diagrams and images. There's no subscription, no credits and no message limit, and if it isn't right for you there's a full refund within 15 days (email support@greencube.app).

When a no-setup app isn't the right choice

  • Older or low-memory PCs. GreenCube runs on about 8 GB of RAM with no graphics card, but slowly: 5–7 tokens a second on the PCs we tested, about reading speed. Making images needs 12 GB of RAM.
  • The hardest problems. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are smarter on tough maths, code and niche facts.
  • Today's news, unless the app can look things up and you switch that on.
  • Macs. GreenCube is for Windows 10 and 11 only.
  • Control. If you want to choose models and adjust settings, a free tool like LM Studio or Ollama suits you better (see the best local AI apps).

Many people end up with a sensible split: a cloud AI for hard questions with nothing private in them, and a local app for their own documents and for when they're offline.
