If you want a ChatGPT alternative that keeps your files on your own computer, these five are worth a look: Jan (free and open source), GreenCube (€9.99 once, the simplest on Windows), Elephas (a Mac app on a subscription), Sanctum (a private desktop app for open models) and Rana (a browser-based project for technical users). All of them are smaller than ChatGPT, so they trade some smarts for privacy and offline use.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. I've kept this list to tools that run AI on your own device, because that's the main reason people look for a ChatGPT alternative on this blog. If you just want a different cloud chatbot, Claude and Gemini are the obvious ones; I compare them with GreenCube in Claude vs GreenCube and Gemini vs GreenCube.

Jan

Jan is a free, open-source desktop app for chatting with AI models on your own computer. You download a model from its list and chat with it offline, and it can also run a local server that copies OpenAI's API, so other tools can use it as a drop-in replacement. You can connect it to cloud models too, if you want both in one place.

It suits people who don't mind picking a model and a size. The catch is that choice: if you've never heard of model files or quantisation, expect some reading before it feels easy.

GreenCube

GreenCube is the app I build, for people who want a private AI without the setup. You install it on Windows, it checks your PC and downloads the model that fits (Cube 2B on smaller PCs, Cube 4B with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma), and then it works offline. Your chats, files and images are saved on your PC.

Besides chat, it makes slide decks that save as PowerPoint, quizzes, flashcards, labelled diagrams and pictures, and it reads PDFs, Word files and photos. It costs €9.99 (US$9.99) once, with no subscription or message limits. The catches: Windows only, you sign in once so it can check you paid, and a slow PC is slow (on the PCs we tested, an 8 GB laptop with no graphics card writes at about reading speed).

Elephas

Elephas is an AI assistant for Mac, with iPhone and iPad apps. Its strength is working inside the apps you already use, rewriting, summarising and drafting where you type, and building a searchable knowledge base from your documents. You can run it with models on your Mac or connect it to cloud AI providers.

It suits professionals who live on a Mac and handle a lot of documents. The catches are that it's Mac only and sold as a subscription, and that it's only fully private when you use the on-device mode.

Sanctum

Sanctum is a desktop app for running open-source models privately on Mac and Windows, with your chats and documents kept in an encrypted local store. You can chat with PDFs and switch between models.

It suits people who want a tidy private app and like choosing models. The catch is that there's little independent coverage of it, so check the current platforms and price on its site before you commit.

Rana

Rana takes an unusual approach: its engine runs AI models inside your web browser, on devices you already own, with nothing to install, and the company says your data stays on those devices. It comes with its own chatbot and tools for developers.

It suits technical users who enjoy trying new projects. The catch is that almost everything written about it comes from the company itself, and it's more of a platform to build on than a ready-made ChatGPT replacement.

What you give up compared with ChatGPT

Every tool here runs a model small enough for your own hardware, and that's a real step down from ChatGPT on the hardest problems: long maths, tricky code, very niche facts. None of them know today's news unless they can search the web. What you get back is an AI that works offline, keeps your chats on your own machine, and doesn't run out of messages.
