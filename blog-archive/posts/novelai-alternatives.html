If you want a NovelAI alternative that runs on your own Windows PC instead of a subscription server, there are two routes. For story writing, free local apps like KoboldCpp, SillyTavern or LM Studio run an open model on your machine; for images, Stable Diffusion through ComfyUI or Fooocus does the same. If you'd rather not set anything up, GreenCube is a paid app (€9.99 once) that writes and makes images offline. Every local option is less polished for long fiction than NovelAI, and a slow PC is slow.

I build GreenCube, so weigh my opinion with that in mind. Below is what each route is good for and what it costs you.

Why people look beyond NovelAI

NovelAI is a subscription service for AI-assisted story writing and anime-style images. It runs on NovelAI's servers, so you pay every month, you need a connection, and your stories live on someone else's computer. People who leave usually want one of three things: no monthly bill, the ability to write with the Wi-Fi off, or their drafts kept on their own disk.

A local app gives you all three. You download a model once and it runs on your PC. What you give up is NovelAI's tuning for fiction and its story tools, and you take on the setup and the hardware limits yourself.

Local options for writing

KoboldCpp

A free, single-file program built on llama.cpp that runs GGUF models and comes with a writing-oriented interface, including story and adventure modes. It's the closest thing to a self-hosted NovelAI for text. You pick and download the model yourself, which means reading up on model sizes first (our GGUF guide helps).

SillyTavern

A free front end popular for character chat and roleplay. It doesn't run models itself; you connect it to a back end such as KoboldCpp or Ollama. Very flexible, with a learning curve to match.

LM Studio, Jan, GPT4All

General local chat apps with a friendlier setup. They're fine for drafting scenes and brainstorming, but they lack fiction-specific tools like a lorebook. See GPT4All alternatives and LM Studio alternatives.

Local options for images

Stable Diffusion models run on your own graphics card through free apps such as ComfyUI (node-based, powerful, fiddly) or Fooocus (simpler). Community models cover anime styles too. You want a graphics card with a decent amount of memory for this; on a laptop without one it's slow. Our local image generator speed post has real timings.

GreenCube

GreenCube is for people who want the result without the setup. You install it on Windows, sign in once (only to check you paid), and it picks a model that fits your PC: Cube 2B on smaller laptops, Cube 4B on PCs with a graphics card, both built on Google's Gemma. After that it works offline, and your chats and images are saved on your computer.

It writes, chats and makes images on the PC. An image takes about 2 minutes on a 16 GB laptop with Intel graphics, and images need 12 GB of RAM. It costs €9.99 once, with a 15-day refund. The honest catch for fiction writers: it's a general assistant, not a story engine. It doesn't have NovelAI's story tools such as a lorebook, and a small model loses track of a long plot sooner than the big cloud ones.

What your PC needs

For writing, memory decides which model you can run and speed decides how long you wait. On the PCs we tested, GreenCube writes 5 to 7 tokens a second on an 8 GB laptop without a graphics card (about reading speed), 14 to 18 on a 16 GB laptop, and 37 to 76 on most gaming PCs with a 6 GB or bigger card. Other local apps running similar-sized models are in the same range. Bigger models write better prose but need more memory; see hardware for local AI or check yours with Can my PC run AI?

What local does and doesn't protect

When the model runs on your PC, there's no server holding your drafts. But your own machine now matters: an unencrypted laptop that gets stolen, a shared Windows account, or a backup service that syncs your documents folder can all expose what you wrote. Turn on BitLocker or device encryption, use your own account, and know where your backups go.
