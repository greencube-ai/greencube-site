II — Examination

A small object you can hold in your machine.

Fig. I  Topology of a local mind
i.
Open weights, ≈5.7 GB.

Phi-4, Qwen 3.5, Gemma 4. A single file you actually own. No keys to revoke, no servers to wait on.

ii.
Lives in memory, not on a server.

The model loads onto your CPU and GPU. Conversations sit on your hard drive. Nothing to leak because nothing leaves.

iii.
Reads your hardware. Picks the right model.

On first launch it measures your RAM and GPU and downloads the strongest open model that fits. No tier to pick. No settings to tune.

iv.
No ledger. No quotas.

No accounts. No tokens metered. No analytics dialing home. Run it for an hour or for a week — the only ceiling is your machine.

III — Field notes

Two small claims, on which the rest of this rests.

i. on where a thought lives
A model on your machine is not a smaller version of a model in the cloud. It is a different kind of object entirely.

It does not require a network. It does not log your prompts. It cannot be deprecated, paywalled, or quietly aligned away from you. The same file, on your disk, will still answer your question in five years — even if the company that trained it is gone. That is a strange and rare property, and it is worth caring about.

ii. on the question of who reads it
"Private mode" is not the same thing as private.

Every cloud assistant is, from a privacy standpoint, a stranger you've handed a stack of letters and asked to read aloud only to you. That arrangement is fine until it isn't. GreenCube takes the stranger out of the room. Your conversations stay where you wrote them — on the same hard drive that holds the rest of your life.
