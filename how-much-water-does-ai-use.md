The short answer

## It depends who you ask.

**0.26 mL** per Gemini prompt, says Google (2025). About five drops.

**10–25 mL** per ChatGPT question, says UC Riverside (2023), counting the water behind the electricity.

**700000 L** to train GPT-3 once, says the same UC Riverside study.

Sources: [Google (2025)](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/infrastructure/measuring-the-environmental-impact-of-ai-inference/) · [UC Riverside, “Making AI Less Thirsty” (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03271).

Why water?

## Two places it goes.

### Cooling

Data centres get hot. Many cool down by evaporating fresh water, and that water doesn’t come back.

### Electricity

Power plants use water too. Counting it can make the number around ten times bigger, which is why estimates disagree.

### Where and when

A hot, dry place in summer uses far more water than a cold one in winter. Same question, different answer.

What helps

## What actually reduces it.

### Cooler places, reused water

Building data centres where it’s cold, and cooling them with water that gets reused instead of evaporated.

### Cleaner electricity

Wind and solar use far less water than coal, gas or nuclear plants.

### Smaller models, on devices you own

A model that fits on your PC needs no data-centre cooling at all. That’s GreenCube.

Questions

## Quick answers.

### How much water does one AI question use?

Between a few drops and a couple of tablespoons, depending on who measures. Google says 0.26 mL for a Gemini prompt; UC Riverside estimated about 10 to 25 mL per ChatGPT question.

### Why do the numbers disagree so much?

Some only count the cooling water in the data centre; others also count the water used to make the electricity. Location and season change it too.

### How does GreenCube count water saved?

15 mL for every message you send on GreenCube, the middle of UC Riverside’s range. It’s an estimate, and we say so in the app.

### Does GreenCube use no water at all?

It uses no data-centre cooling water. Your PC still uses electricity, and making electricity uses some water.
